A Failed Multi-Regime ePC Frontier Preflight

Motivation, construction, debugging history, numerical results, and reproducibility record

RelaLeap technical report · 4 August 2026 · Benjamin Goertzel and the RelaLeap research-agent workflow

Result in one sentence. The experiment successfully exercised deterministic T=1/T=2/T=4 training and monotone ePC activity settlement, but the matched T=1 positive control failed the predeclared learnability floor in every evaluation regime on both final seeds; therefore the task was not admitted and no claim about a diverse ePC Pareto frontier, expert specialization, or mixture-of-experts benefit was made.

Abstract

The motivating question was whether the previously tested ePC family contained multiple diversely good configurations: models that were all viable overall but had stable, complementary conditional strengths. Such a population could motivate a later mixture-of-experts router. A preceding two-context/two-mode toy experiment had found conditional differences, but they disappeared under held-out confirmation. One plausible explanation was that both the network and task were too small to provide distinct functional niches.

We therefore constructed a larger semantic-free synthetic sequence preflight with four contexts, two continuation modes, two candidate caps, and four independently scored regimes: in-distribution continuation, compositional remapping, nuisance-cue perturbation, and replay-supported sequential adaptation with retention. A two-layer GPT-2-style causal transformer was trained under a matched direct endpoint (T=1) and two predictive-coding settlement depths (T=2 and T=4). The experiment was deliberately fail-closed: ePC diversity was not interpretable unless the matched T=1 control first achieved negative log-likelihood (NLL) at most 1.4 in every regime on every seed.

The execution history contained useful failures. The first direct invocation had an import-path error. The first executable run revealed a PyTorch advanced-indexing bug that left the teacher logits unchanged. After correcting it, an adaptation-only schedule caused catastrophic forgetting. Adding replay and a planted mode-to-cap positive control still did not make the high-vocabulary fixture learnable. A final 64-token calibration also failed: final T=1 NLLs ranged from 6.76 to 11.92 across regimes, far above 1.4. All final arm replays were exact, all T=2/T=4 energy traces were monotone, and the full source test suite passed. Thus the failure was not numerical nondeterminism, but it also was not evidence against ePC: the benchmark itself never cleared its matched-control gate.

1. Motivation

1.1 From average quality to conditional complementarity

An MoE is scientifically motivated only when candidate experts are each good enough to use and reliably win in different regimes. A conventional Pareto frontier over aggregate loss, representation rank, or diversity can be misleading: a configuration may have an important conditional advantage that disappears under averaging, while another may appear diverse merely because it fails differently.

The intended admission logic was therefore:

  1. Viability: every candidate must meet absolute task-quality and stability floors and remain close to a matched T=1 baseline.
  2. Nondominance: conditional regime losses must be retained as separate Pareto axes.
  3. Complementarity: two candidates must exhibit bidirectional wins—A beats B in at least one regime and B beats A in at least one other regime—repeated over untouched confirmation seeds.
  4. Reproducibility: complete reruns from identical initial model, optimizer, and random-number states must match exactly; ePC activity energy must be non-increasing.

The preceding tiny fixture had only two contexts, two modes, and four-token continuations. Its repaired frontier screen retained two exploration candidates, one selection candidate, and zero confirmation candidates. The follow-up reported here asked whether a richer task would expose stable niches that the tiny fixture could not.

1.2 Why a preflight rather than an immediate frontier sweep

A conditional frontier is meaningless if the benchmark is not solvable by a matched ordinary learner. Accordingly, this run was a construction preflight, not the full exploration/selection/confirmation sweep. The gate required the T=1 control to solve all regimes first. Only after that pass would ePC configurations be compared or promoted.

2. Experimental design

2.1 Synthetic sequence language

Each example contains a regime token, context token, candidate-cap token, nuisance-cue token, and three blank tokens. Model logits at four continuation positions are evaluated against a planted four-token target. The final fixture has:

  • 4 contexts and 2 modes;
  • 2 candidate caps, with a transparent mode-to-cap mapping used only as a positive control;
  • 4-token continuations;
  • a 64-token vocabulary;
  • deterministically generated target tables salted separately by regime.

The target token at context c, mode m, continuation position p, and regime salt s is

token(c,m,p;s) = 16 + ((s + 7c + 3m + 5p) mod 48).

The regime salts are 0, 11, 23, and 37. Adaptation shifts the last mapping by a further 17 modulo 48.

2.3 Model and training

The model is a dropout-free Hugging Face GPT2LMHeadModel with 2 residual blocks, embedding width 32, 2 attention heads, and inner width 64. The two candidate caps are evaluated from the same context/regime example. For the positive-control fixture, cap 0 receives mode-0 targets and cap 1 receives mode-1 targets. This is intentionally easier than learned routing: it tests whether the model/regime construction is learnable before routing is studied.

All arms begin from identical initial weights for a given seed and use AdamW with learning rate 0.003 and zero weight decay. Final r5 uses 192 pretraining updates over ID/composition/perturbation examples followed by 96 adaptation updates mixing shifted-adaptation and original-ID replay examples. Batch size is 32; evaluation size is 192.

2.4 T=1 and ePC objectives

T=1 is the direct knowledge-distillation endpoint: the model is differentiated through its feed-forward logits against the constructed teacher distribution. T>1 exposes the residual-stream state after each GPT-2 block and performs activity inference before differentiating the settled local energy.

For block predictions fl, settled activities zl, input activity z0, and teacher distribution q, the implemented local energy is

E(z) = 1/2 Σ_l mean || z_l - f_l(z_{l-1}) ||²
       + λ KL(q || softmax(logits(z_L)/τ)),

with output weight λ=1, temperature τ=1, and initial relaxation step 0.2. Backtracking accepts an activity update only when the energy does not increase beyond a small floating-point tolerance. T=2 and T=4 denote the number of recorded activity-settlement evaluations, including the initial state.

2.5 Frozen gate and reproducibility checks

The preflight control passes only if, for every final seed:

  • T=1 replay is bit-exact and its energy trace is valid;
  • every one of id, compose, perturb, adapt, and retain_id has NLL ≤ 1.4.

Each T=1/T=2/T=4 arm is independently trained twice from the same initial state and deterministic batches. Model-state SHA-256 digests and the complete returned records must agree. Any ePC arm must also report monotone activity energy. Failure of T=1 terminates the experiment before conditional Pareto interpretation.

3.1 The r2 PyTorch indexing defect

The original target-writing code selected winners using advanced indexing and then called an in-place operation on the selected object. In PyTorch, advanced indexing returns a copy rather than a view; consequently the constructed one-hot logits did not propagate back into the full teacher tensor. The student was trained against its own unchanged logits and stayed at chance. The repair clones the selected candidate rows, writes the target logits, and explicitly assigns those rows back. A unit test now verifies that exactly one candidate has maximum teacher logit 12 at every continuation position and that its argmax sequence equals the planted target.

3.2 Why r3 and r4 were not ePC results

R3 showed the repaired pathway could move the model: T=1 adaptation NLL fell to 3.274 and 3.055, while prior-regime NLLs were 6.425–8.327. But adaptation-only training made retention structurally unrealistic. R4 added matched ID replay and explicit mode-to-cap ownership; its T=1 adaptation NLL was 4.698 and 4.704, while other metrics remained 5.269–7.954. Because the ordinary control still failed, neither run licenses a T=1 versus ePC comparison.

5. What failed—and what did not

Observed final failure: the T=1 control failed every absolute NLL floor on both seeds. The benchmark therefore did not establish a viable larger task.

Observed successes: deterministic seed handling, exact replay, model-digest capture, four-regime metric accounting, and monotone ePC settlement all operated as intended in r5. The complete RelaLeap test suite passed 208 tests after implementation.

Not established: the run does not show that ePC cannot sustain stable specialization. It does not compare trained viable models, does not produce a Pareto frontier, does not evaluate a router, and does not address language-scale performance. It only shows that this particular planted fixture and bounded CPU schedule failed its required ordinary-control gate.

5.1 Plausible explanations

  • The fixture may remain too sparse relative to model width and update count, despite vocabulary reduction.
  • Simultaneously fitting distinct salted token maps may create arbitrary interference rather than meaningful compositional structure.
  • The positive-control cap assignment guarantees mode identity but does not itself make the multiple maps easy to learn.
  • The KD objective averages across all sequence positions and both candidates, while only one candidate's continuation positions receive one-hot targets, potentially diluting the effective training signal.
  • The adaptation/replay mixture may still be poorly balanced for joint acquisition and retention.

These are hypotheses. Resolving them would require a prospectively specified successor using fresh seeds. Tuning further on 41001/41002 would turn the final gate into calibration data and cannot validate the current contract.

7. Recommended successor

A successor should first validate its measurement and training instrument on analytically simple cases, then use a task with meaningful shared structure rather than unrelated salted lookup tables. The ordinary learner should pass prospectively frozen ID/composition/perturbation/adaptation/retention gates on fresh calibration seeds before any ePC grid opens. Only then should the experiment freeze exploration, selection, and confirmation splits and test bidirectional conditional crossover. Model scaling alone is insufficient; task structure, control learnability, and a non-diluted training objective are the immediate design priorities.
