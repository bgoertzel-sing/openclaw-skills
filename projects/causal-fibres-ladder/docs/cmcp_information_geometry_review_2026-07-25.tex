\begin{abstract}
CMCP is an evidence ledger for test-time constraint packets. Its original weight is a Schur-complement residual in a score-vector representation. Experiments establish useful accounting and replay-budget effects, but reveal a central limitation: a provenance-distinct systematic bit-flip can be almost fully novel in that linear score space. Distance-correlation novelty plus a Schweizer--Sklar t-norm suppresses this constructed bias, but calibration selects the product limit rather than a non-product t-norm. This review separates those observations from a proposed conditional distributional increment based on alpha divergence, with Fisher--Rao/Hellinger as the symmetric primary. The proposal is falsifiable; it is not established conditional mutual information or a guaranteed solution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and current CMCP}
CMCP packets contain a batch-aligned constraint-value vector, mechanism family, provenance/innovation identifiers, and a precision weight. Its role is to decide how much extra statistical weight a packet deserves given a ledger of stored packets. In reduced E4, this weight controls direct-logit constraint settlement and, in the parameter-learning follow-up, replay strength.

For a candidate packet $j$, let $s_j\in\mathbb{R}^d$ be its standardized score residual. With stored scores $S=\{s_i\}$, the original conditional ratio is
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{score}}(j\mid S)=
\frac{F_{jj}-F_{jS}(F_{SS}+\lambda I)^\dagger F_{Sj}}{F_{jj}},
\qquad F_{ab}=d^{-1}s_a^\top s_b,
\label{eq:schur}
\end{equation}
clipped to $[0,1]$. It is zero for a score explained by the stored linear span and near one for an orthogonal score. A prior provenance gate selects one stable representative per innovation identifier.

This criterion has genuine strengths: exact duplicate/descendant idempotence, retention of independent repeated evidence, and tested equivariance under supplied orthogonal score-coordinate alignment. But it is a linear conditional-energy criterion on selected features. It is neither general conditional mutual information nor a test for arbitrary deterministic dependence.

\section{What was observed}
\subsection{Accounting and persistent bursts}
Five held-out single-shot confirmation seeds gave exact idempotence for exact duplicates and deterministic descendants, mean weight $0.997862$ for an independent second packet, and order invariance after canonicalization. Partial redundancy was high: $0.657531$ versus constructed oracle $0.5$. Naively counting a duplicate twice slightly improved immediate loss; the initial result was accounting, not performance evidence.

The corrected eight-episode persistent experiment reused one explicitly identified cohort and changed extraction noise by episode. An earlier fresh-batch smoke was rejected because row-aligned packets were applied to unrelated examples. On five sealed confirmation seeds at episode 8:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}\toprule
Metric & CMCP & oracle & naive accumulation\\\midrule
Effective precision & 8.89433 & 9.0 & 26.0\\
Task accuracy & 0.83281 & --- & 0.74844\\
ECE (10-bin) & 0.07797 & --- & 0.12309\\
Multiclass Brier score & 0.22596 & --- & 0.32489\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\obs CMCP prevented duplicate count from becoming precision inflation and improved same-cohort accuracy/calibration in this frozen-weight protocol.

\subsection{Parameter learning and replay}
A typed store now carries cohort and ordered-example identity, packet/provenance/innovation identity, mechanism family, precision, and values hash. It fails closed on row permutation, cohort mismatch, duplicate packet IDs, and conflicting innovation payloads. In a three-seed six-episode reversal, CMCP precision was $6.8364$--$7.9343$ against oracle $7.5$, while naive reached $21.0$. Yet naive retained Task A better on every seed and usually matched Task-B learning; five confirmation seeds were not opened.

A replay-coefficient sweep produced a bounded plasticity signal. Increasing coefficient from $0.15$ to $0.30$ to $0.50$ moved mean CMCP/naive Task-B crossover from episode 6 to 5 to 3. At episode 6, CMCP minus naive Task-B accuracy was $+0.031$, $+0.099$, and $+0.133$. This supports CMCP as a replay-budget normalizer under stronger replay pressure. It does not establish a universal continual-learning win; naive had lower ECE in this exploratory sweep.

\subsection{The technical failure}
In a provenance-distinct systematic bit-flip smoke, raw score-space ratio was $0.9461$: nearly fully novel under Equation \ref{eq:schur}. Thus original score-space CMCP did not recognize the deterministic transformation. Earlier zero weight in a related test was explained by shared provenance, not by the Schur test. A deterministic systematic transformation can look linearly independent in chosen score features.

\section{Schweizer--Sklar result}
The prototype used raw-evidence novelty
\begin{equation}
r_{\mathrm{ev}}(j\mid S)=1-\max_{i\in S}\operatorname{dCor}(v_j,v_i),
\end{equation}
where dCor is the implemented finite-sample biased Szekely--Rizzo distance correlation, averaged factorwise. It combines score and evidence novelty by
\begin{equation}
T_p(x,y)=\left[\max\{0,x^p+y^p-1\}\right]^{1/p},\quad p>0,\qquad
r_Q=T_p(r_{\mathrm{score}},r_{\mathrm{ev}}).
\label{eq:ss}
\end{equation}
As $p\to0^+$, this tends to product $xy$; $p=1$ is the Lukasiewicz t-norm.

On three calibration seeds with $p\in[0.05,1.5]$, selected endpoint was $p=0.05$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrr}\toprule
Fixture & exact duplicate & bit-flip & independent & 50\% overlap\\\midrule
Mean $r_Q$ & 0.000 & 0.000 & 0.870930 & 0.363021\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The selection score fell monotonically from $0.833950$ at $p=0.05$ to $0.553181$ at $p=1.5$. An auxiliary product-limit computation ($p=0.001$) was slightly better on the same constructed objective. A provenance-distinct bit-flip smoke assigned $1.0/0.0$ to original/biased packets with model weights unchanged.

\obs Evidence dependence catches this constructed complement relation. The data supports a product-like score/evidence combination, not a specifically non-product Schweizer--Sklar geometry: the optimum is at a tested boundary. Biased finite-sample dCor and partial-overlap target $0.4$ are not independently calibrated truths.

\section{Why not standard MI?}
Mutual information requires a specified joint distribution over packets, targets/parameters, and conditioning ledger. Equation \ref{eq:schur} is a useful Gaussian-linear analogue, not general MI. A neural MI estimator would introduce a learned critic, substantial sample requirements, and a new route for fixture overfit.

A more disciplined next object is an \emph{information-geometric distributional increment}: assess packets by the predictive distributions they induce on a fixed typed cohort. This is a testable estimator, not conditional MI unless a posterior or generative model makes that identification valid.

\section{Proposed math: conditional distributional novelty}
Fix a cohort $C$ with ordered example identity. Let $P_0(c)$ be feed-forward categorical prediction, $P_S(c)$ prediction after stored packets $S$, $P_j(c)$ after candidate $j$ alone, and $P_{S\cup\{j\}}(c)$ after ledger plus candidate. All four are computed by frozen-weight direct-logit settlement.

Let $\bar D_\alpha$ be cohort-mean Amari alpha divergence:
\begin{equation}
D_\alpha(P\Vert Q)=\frac{4}{1-\alpha^2}
\left[1-\sum_kP_k^{(1-\alpha)/2}Q_k^{(1+\alpha)/2}\right],
\quad -1<\alpha<1.
\label{eq:alpha}
\end{equation}
Its endpoints are directed KL divergences. At $\alpha=0$, $D_0=4(1-\sum_k\sqrt{P_kQ_k})$, four times squared Hellinger distance. This symmetric case is bounded for categorical predictions and locally induces the Fisher metric, making it a sound primary geometry.

Define operational conditional novelty by
\begin{equation}
r_\alpha^{\mathrm{geom}}(j\mid S)=
\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
\frac{\bar D_\alpha(P_S\Vert P_{S\cup\{j\}})}
{\bar D_\alpha(P_0\Vert P_j)+\epsilon}.
\label{eq:ratio}
\end{equation}
The numerator is residual predictive displacement after the ledger; denominator is candidate stand-alone displacement. No additional settled predictive change gives zero; disjoint effects should give a ratio near one.

For a kernel formulation define alpha affinity
\begin{equation}
K_\alpha(P,Q)=|C|^{-1}\sum_{c\in C}\sum_k
P_k(c)^{(1-\alpha)/2}Q_k(c)^{(1+\alpha)/2}.
\end{equation}
At $\alpha=0$, this is Bhattacharyya/Hellinger affinity: a positive-definite kernel on square-root probability vectors and directly Fisher--Rao motivated. Nonzero alpha is best treated as a directed divergence family, not casually as a symmetric kernel.

\hypo A bit flip can be linearly orthogonal in present score features yet produce little residual \emph{predictive} displacement after source constraint is active. Equation \ref{eq:ratio} would then be low where Schur residualization was $0.9461$. Unlike raw dCor, it tests the model response to the packet intervention.

This is not guaranteed. If bit-flip constraint genuinely moves predictions in a distinct direction, Fisher--Rao/alpha geometry should report novelty despite deterministic raw relation. Then suitable policy may require both typed provenance/dependence and geometric predictive increment. The proposal must therefore face counterexamples and held-out confirmation.

\section{Falsification-first protocol}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Estimator audit:} stable log-space alpha divergence, endpoint KL limits, Hellinger primary, and permutation or unbiased dependence baselines. Test non-negativity, identity, zero-probability stability, cohort-order invariance, frozen weights.
\item \textbf{Broaden fixtures:} exact copies, bit flips, factor permutations, cross-factor deterministic maps, affine/logit maps, noisy copies across SNR, and independent packets.
\item \textbf{Compare estimators:} linear Schur, product+dCor, Hellinger ratio, alpha grid. Calibration selects primary and thresholds; product remains a baseline.
\item \textbf{Seal confirmation:} fresh seeds and fresh transform mixtures; require both discrimination and downstream replay-budget plasticity at fixed update counts.
\item \textbf{Defer learning:} any learned copula or critic trains on a separate fixture-generating distribution and is evaluated on held-out mechanism families while retaining Hellinger/product baselines and typed-cohort contracts.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Questions for reviewers}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Is settled predictive displacement the right object, or must packet information first be defined by a posterior or likelihood model?
\item Should primary geometry be symmetric Hellinger/Fisher--Rao ($\alpha=0$), or directed KL because updates are asymmetric?
\item Does direct-logit settlement define a meaningful intervention, or should comparison live in parameter, natural-gradient, or belief space?
\item Which invariances should a kernel respect: factor relabeling, known rotations, deterministic invertible maps, cohort permutation?
\item Can this ratio distinguish useful correlated repetition from redundant repetition without silently preferring novelty?
\item What held-out fixtures would rule out a learned kernel merely memorizing the present grammar?
\end{enumerate}

\section{Current decision}
\proposal Do not replace CMCP in the main path yet. Retain provenance deduplication and product-like dCor as exploratory baselines. Implement Equation \ref{eq:ratio} as a separate preregistered estimator comparison with $\alpha=0$ Hellinger primary; treat nonzero-alpha selection as empirical, not a consequence of information geometry. A learned kernel is premature until a broad held-out fixture distribution and reliable geometric baseline exist.
