                                              Abstract
         These notes supply the mathematical backbone for the July 2026 upgrade of the Chaos
     Language Algorithm (CLA) and its chaoslang implementation. Part I gives the new adap-
     tive/prequential MDL objective an exact coding-theoretic semantics, including the implicit
     prior of the one-part inline code. Part II proves where code-length differences can come
     from at all (the Invariance Lemma), derives a recurrence-time ceiling showing that exact-
     repeat chunk grammars are confined to the Lempel–Ziv redundancy class on positive-entropy
     sources, and establishes a separation theorem locating the genuine home turf of grammar
     codes: hierarchically (substitution-)generated sources, where grammar codes beat every
     Markov, context-tree, and LZ control by polylogarithmic versus nearly-linear total bits.
     Part III develops the automata track: the ε-machine is the MDL-optimal grammar of a
     sofic source, MDL category acceptance is a consistent test of contextual exchangeability,
     and—combining the two—CLA meta-symbols on sofic sources are exactly the fibers of the
     alphabet’s action on causal states, i.e. kernel classes of the syntactic-monoid map. Part IV
     corrects and sharpens the McBride-derivative/quantale bridge: in the additive quantale the
     McBride derivative of a proposal is the exact MDL first difference, emergent synergy is the
     mixed second difference (supermodular edit interaction), and a closed-form synergy estima-
     tor yields a principled composite-proposal generator. Two new falsifiable predictions follow:
     chunk-grammar CLA should win decisively on substitution-structured dynamical regimes
     (period-doubling accumulation points, Sturmian codings) and lose to causal-state coding
     on fully hyperbolic chaos—reframing CLA as a detector of hierarchical generativity rather
     than of chaos per se. Standard results are cited as [std]; project-specific claims carry proof
     sketches.

2    The Invariance Lemma: where can bits be saved?
Chunk rewriting is invertible: given the grammar, the rewritten stream determines S1:n and
conversely. The same holds for category rewriting with recorded members M [v], and for the
factorization of a category-bearing stream into (class stream, member choices) via the chain
rule.

Lemma 1 (Invariance). Let TG be any invertible grammar-rewriting transformation. Then
H(TG (S1:n )) = H(S1:n ). Consequently, for any lossless coding scheme the expected total code
length of a grammar arm satisfies E ℓ ≥ H(S1:n ), and the difference between two lossless arms
(grammar versus no-grammar, both using ideal-bits prequential coders) is a difference of redun-
dancies plus model/definition costs only:

                                   E ∆ℓ = ∆ρn + ∆Lmodel .

Proof. Entropy is invariant under bijections; the lower bound is the noiseless coding theorem;
subtract.

Remark. No grammar edit can beat the entropy of the data; a grammar “helps” exactly insofar
as the rewritten stream is cheaper for the frozen base coder to learn than the raw stream, i.e.
insofar as rewriting reduces the effective parametric complexity (number and size of contexts,
memory length) of the best predictive model, or shortens the learning transient. This single
lemma explains three of the central July findings.

1. The 65,536-symbol attractor failures. Microstate streams of hyperbolic-like flows are
   close to Markov (Sections 7–9): the raw stream is already in the base coder’s low-redundancy
   class, so a redundancy reduction by rewriting is unavailable and any positive model cost loses.
   More data can never fix this.

2. The Fibonacci/paperfolding paradox (suffix code improved, total lost): rewriting by sub-
   stitution rules genuinely shortens the effective memory (large −∆ρn ), but the July two-part
   model code charged parameters at static rates, swamping the gain. Under adaptive mem-
   ber/parameter codes this charge collapses to the k2 log n scale (Section 4); this is the mecha-
   nistic content of prediction P4 of the evaluation programme.

3. The objective is not “compression” naively. Two arms with identical asymptotic rates
   can differ by Θ(log n) or Θ(n/ log n) redundancy; that, not the rate, is the entire battlefield
   on stationary sources.


3    What chunking can buy: the recurrence-time ceiling
Proposition 1 (Chunk-length ceiling). Let h > 0. By the Ornstein–Weiss return-time theorem
[std], the first return time Rℓ of an ℓ-block satisfies 1ℓ log2 Rℓ → h almost surely. Hence in a

 stream of length n, blocks of length ℓ ≫ h1 log2 n typically occur once; every chunk rule (which
 requires at least two occurrences) has body length O(h−1 log n), and the grammar’s dictionary
 behaves like an LZ-class parse.

 Corollary 1 (Chunk grammars are confined to the LZ redundancy class). For stationary ergodic
 S with h > 0, any exact-repeat chunk grammar code (under any search) has ρn = Ω(n/ log n) in
 the worst case over the class, matching LZ up to constants; whereas if S is additionally UHMM
 with s states, the CausalStateCoder attains ρn = O(log n).

 Proof sketch. The grammar code’s data part is a parsing into dictionary phrases of length
 O(log n) by Proposition 1; phrase-counting converse bounds for dictionary codes on ergodic
 sources give the Ω(n/ log n) redundancy floor [std]. The UHMM upper bound is the Causal-
 StateCoder row of the table in Section 1.

     This is the formal version of “chaos gives statistical, not verbatim, regularity,” and it li-
 censes the partial-outcome clause of experiment E7: on hyperbolic-regime fixtures, chunk-CLA
 cannot beat CSSR/CTW except by constants; a loss there is a theorem-level expectation, not
 an implementation bug.

 Proposition 2 (Separation theorem: the home turf of grammar codes). Let S ∈ SUB be a
 linearly recurrent substitution word (e.g. the Fibonacci word, factor complexity p(k) = k + 1).
 Then:

 (i) it admits a straight-line program (SLP) / grammar of size O(log n), so the inline grammar
     code of Section 5 achieves total length O(log2 n);

(ii) every order-k Markov coder pays total at least c n/k bits: there is a right-special factor
     of every length, whose frequency under the unique ergodic measure is Θ(1/k), contributing
     hk = Θ(1/k); optimizing k against the mk log n parameter cost leaves total cost Ω̃(n/ log n);

(iii) LZ78 pays Θ(n/ log n) (phrase counting on zero-entropy sequences [std]).

 Hence grammar codes separate from all Markov/CTW/LZ controls by polylog(n) versus Θ̃(n/ log n)
 exactly on hierarchically generated sources.

 Remark (Scientific positioning). The algorithm’s discriminating power is not “chaos detec-
 tion” but detection of hierarchical generativity: CLA should win precisely on substitution-
 structured dynamics and lose (to parametric automata coders) on fully developed hyperbolic
 chaos. Section 9 turns this into two new falsifiable fixtures.

 Proposition 3 (Hardness, and an initializer with a guarantee). The smallest-grammar problem
 is NP-hard and APX-hard [std: Charikar, Lehman, Liu, Panigrahy, Prabhakaran, Sahai, Shelat
 2005]; greedy and Re-Pair have super-constant worst-case approximation ratios, while an LZ77-
 factorization-based balanced-SLP construction achieves approximation ratio O(log(n/g ∗ )) to the
 smallest grammar size g ∗ [std: Rytter 2003; Charikar et al.].

 Remark (Engineering consequence). Add LZ77SLPInitializer alongside RePairInitializer
 in milestone M-D: it is the only initializer with a worst-case guarantee, and on SUB-class fixtures
 the guarantee is meaningful (g ∗ = O(log n)). It also calibrates expectations for experiment E4:
 search approximates an NP-hard optimum, so the 80%-gap-closure gate is the right relative
 criterion.

6    Category edits as parameter tying: a consistency theorem
Fix a frame occurrence set for candidate M = {v1 , . . . , vr }. Say the members are contextually
exchangeable if for all pasts p and futures f occurring with the frame,

                  P (f | p, member = vi ) = P (f | p, member = vj )                 ∀ i, j.       (CE)

The category-bearing code factors each occurrence into (class token M , member choice), coding
the class token in the base coder’s stream and the member with a per-category adaptive KT.

Theorem 1 (Consistency of category acceptance). Under the adaptive scorer, with the frame
occurring νn → ∞ times:

(a) If (CE) holds, the category edit ties the r per-member successor distributions into one, reduc-
    ing the free-parameter count by ∆k = (r−1)·(successor df) at the price of the member code ≈
    νn H(member)+ r−1 2 log νn . Since the member entropy was already being paid inside the untied
    successor distributions (chain rule), the net change is ∆L = − ∆k         r−1
                                                                    2 log n+ 2 log νn +O(1) < 0
    eventually whenever the tying removes at least r degrees of freedom, and the edit is accepted
    a.s. for large n.

(b) If (CE) fails with average conditional divergence D̄ > 0, tying incurs data cost at least
    νn D̄(1 + o(1)), which dominates every log-scale saving; the edit is rejected a.s.

Proof sketch. Both directions are the standard k2 log n versus nD dichotomy of parametric-
mixture code lengths [std], applied to the tied versus untied families; the only CLA-specific
point is the chain-rule bookkeeping showing member-choice entropy is not double-charged.

Remark. MDL category acceptance is a consistent statistical test of contextual exchangeabil-
ity. This upgrades the Jensen–Shannon threshold heuristic (a fixed θ on context divergence,
neither necessary nor sufficient) to a calibrated test, and it says exactly which planted structures
experiments E3/E6 must recover: those where (CE) holds by construction.

Histories x, x′ are predictively equivalent if P (future | x) = P (future | x′ ); causal states are
the equivalence classes; the ε-machine is the induced unifilar, minimal, sufficient presentation
[std: Crutchfield–Young 1989; Shalizi–Crutchfield 2001]. CSSR consistently estimates it for
finite-state sources [std: Shalizi–Klinkner 2004].

for predictively sufficient M, and ℓ(M) ≥ n(h + DM ) + O(log n) with DM > 0 for insufficient
M. Every sufficient unifilar presentation refines the P ε-machine (causal states are the coarsest
sufficient statistic), and refinement cannot decrease σ (|Aσ | − 1): splitting σ into nonempty

    This theorem is the license for the automata track: on sofic symbolic dynamics—which
includes the symbolic dynamics of uniformly hyperbolic systems with Markov partitions [std:
Bowen]—the correct output of a “chaos language” analysis is a finite automaton with transition
probabilities, and the CausalStateCoder is its MDL-optimal code. Chunk/category grammars
are, on this class, at best a re-notation of the same automaton and, by Corollary 1, a worse-coded
one.


8    Categories as syntactic-monoid fibers
Let M be the ε-machine with state set Σ and (partial) unifilar transition δ : Σ × V → Σ. Each
token v induces a partial transformation δv : Σ → Σ; the monoid generated by {δv } is the
transition monoid, which for the minimal presentation of a sofic shift is the syntactic monoid of
its language [std].
Definition 1 (Alphabet congruence). v ≡ w ⇐⇒ δv = δw on all reachable states (equal as
partial maps: same domain of definedness, same images).
Theorem 3 (Category–congruence correspondence).
(a) ≡ is the coarsest equivalence on V such that the quotient process—replace each st by its class
    [st ]—remains unifilar over the class alphabet with the same state set; the member choice at
    each step, conditioned on the current causal state and class, is then predictively irrelevant.
    Thus classes of ≡ satisfy contextual exchangeability (CE) uniformly in every context.
(b) Conversely, any CLA category all of whose occurrences satisfy (CE) in every frame is a
    refinement of an ≡-class on the states where it occurs.
(c) Therefore, on a sofic source, the categories that Theorem 1 accepts in the large-sample limit
    are exactly (unions of occurrences of ) the fibers of the map V → syntactic monoid, v 7→ δv —
    which is precisely the rule implemented by CSSRCategoryInducer (specification Section 5.3,
    with the finite-support caveat).

   holds in every context, then v, w drive every reachable state to predictively equivalent—hence

10     The quantale/weakness bridge, corrected and sharpened
Work in the additive quantale Q = ([0, ∞], ⊗ = +, ⊕ = inf, e = 0) with residuation a\c =
max(c − a, 0). Define the weakness of a model structure as its stochastic complexity: w(G) =
L(G, S) under the adaptive scorer, equivalently − log2 of the Bayes score of Proposition 4.
This replaces the July bridge note’s mixed conventions with a single consistent instantiation;
Definitions 1–2 and Propositions 1–2 of that note survive verbatim under this reading, with the
corrections below.

10.1    Edits as one-hole contexts (now exact)
A proposal q is a one-hole context in the grammar–stream pair; its McBride derivative is the
exact first difference ∂q L = L(G + q, S) − L(G, S) = ∆L(q), computable (not merely estimable)
by the scorer’s delta. Greedy CLA is steepest descent in this discrete gradient field—literally
rather than approximately, because the adaptive scorer’s ∆ has no unaccounted parameter terms.

10.2    Synergy is the mixed second difference

10.4    Correction to the bridge note’s Proposition 3 (the ODE relaxation)
The claim that the grammar trajectory satisfies a quantale exponential-growth ODE requires
∆q(t) ≈ κ ⊗ m(t), i.e. improvement proportional to current weakness, which the acceptance rule
does not guarantee and which ledger data can falsify. The defensible replacements: (i) L(G(t), S)
is nonincreasing and bounded below by H(S1:n ) (Lemma 1), so the trajectory converges; (ii)
the edit lattice with ∆2 L of mixed sign is neither sub- nor supermodular globally, which is
precisely why greedy fails and why beam/composite search is needed. The correct dynamical
statement about CLA’s own search is “gradient descent on a non-modular discrete landscape,”
and the interesting “chaotic grammar dynamics” question (bridge note Open Question 4) be-
comes whether beam-search trajectories on SUB-class inputs exhibit sensitive dependence on
tie-breaking—which the deterministic-seed ledgers measure directly.

10.5    Causal quotients as weakness minimization
Theorem 2 in this language: among predicates mπ induced by partitions π of histories, predic-
tive sufficiency is the pattern condition I(mπ ) ≥ e (zero residual divergence), and the ε-machine
is the coarsest π satisfying it—the weakness-minimizing quotient, since coarsening reduces the
log n-scale weakness term while sufficiency pins the linear term at nh. Theorem 3 then says
the alphabet congruence is the restriction of the same universal construction from histories
to letters. The whole upgraded system is one principle instantiated three times: minimize
stochastic-complexity weakness; chunks descend it syntagmatically, categories de-

11     Open problems
Open Problem 1 (Stochastic-CFG analogue of Theorem 2). Is there an MDL-consistency
theorem selecting a minimal PCFG among sufficient hierarchical presentations of a SUB-class
source, playing the role the ε-machine plays for UHMM? Natural conjecture: yes, with the inline
prior of Proposition 7; the obstruction is non-identifiability of PCFGs.
Open Problem 2 (Rate of Theorem 1). Finite-sample thresholds: how large must νn be, as a
function of D̄ and r, for the correct accept/reject decision with high probability? This would let
experiments E3/E6 predeclare crossover horizons instead of estimating them.
Open Problem 3 (Approximation ratio of composite-beam search). On SUB-class inputs, does
synergy-guided composite/beam search provably improve on the Rytter O(log(n/g ∗ )) baseline
relative to the smallest grammar, or only empirically?
Open Problem 4 (Two-regime front-end classifier for OmegaSim). Find a statistic (e.g. growth
exponent of the chunk dictionary versus n, contrasted with causal-state count stability) that
classifies a trace as sofic-regime versus substitution-regime before committing to a model class,
turning the Section 9 dichotomy into a front-end detector.

Open Problem 5 (Quantale choice, partially resolved). In the additive quantale the theory
above is exact; the probability quantale ([0, 1], ×, max) corresponds to replacing ⊕-aggregation
by best-case (Viterbi-like) scoring, which breaks the mixture semantics of Proposition 4. So the
additive quantale is not merely convenient but forced by the Bayesian reading; the remaining
question is whether any non-additive quantale supports a coherent robust (minimax-regret /
NML) variant.
