\begin{abstract}
This report consolidates the durable CLA evidence through July 23, 2026. It
covers the pure-Python \texttt{chaoslang} implementation, low- and
high-dimensional symbolization, synthetic grammar and complete-code tests,
Mackey--Glass and Lorenz--96 calibration, geometric chaos-detector candidates,
and the complete OmegaSim A6 CLA sweep. The central result is not that every
part of CLA is broken. Exact reconstruction, deterministic replay, frozen
prefix/suffix separation, compact model transmission, and shuffled-null
rejection are generally sound. Low-cardinality symbolization repairs the
obvious high-dimensional alphabet explosion. A separately calibrated
nearest-neighbor divergence detector also discriminated the bounded positive
and control battery. The unresolved failure is the bridge from a dynamical
trajectory to a compact, generalizing grammar: model-description cost is often
larger than the suffix savings, the search tends to return one- or
two-production grammars with no categories, and complete CLA codes lose to
unigram, Markov, or LZ78 controls on the chaotic fixtures. The report separates
direct measurements from interpretation and proposes diagnostic experiments
that can distinguish representation failure, search failure, and MDL
accounting failure before further OmegaSim claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Executive diagnosis}

\textbf{Code URL:} \codeurl.

\textbf{Current scientific status.} CLA is a well-tested experimental
infrastructure and a partially calibrated symbolic coder, not yet a validated
``chaos grammar'' detector. The decisive split is:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Engineering integrity mostly works:} exact reconstruction,
canonical persistence, deterministic replay, prefix-only fitting, and null
controls have repeatedly passed.
\item \textbf{Representation partly works:} direct Cartesian symbolization
fails in high dimension; fitted low-cardinality microstates restore recurrence
and finite-order predictability. Tested linear VAMP embeddings did not beat
matched PCA/raw controls.
\item \textbf{Synthetic grammar competence is conditional:} indexed and
compact transmission can win on some constant-length substitution sources and
at long enough horizons, but does not generalize across all planted families.
\item \textbf{Attractor grammar coding fails:} across scalar, ordinal,
recurrence, and full-vector representations, compact CLA loses to simple
complete-code controls.
\item \textbf{Geometric Stage A can work:} nearest-neighbor divergence v2
passed its six-fixture battery. The subsequent prefix-quantized Stage-B CLA
code lost badly to LZ78.
\item \textbf{OmegaSim is negative under the frozen CLA:} all 120 planned
role-specific A6 cells were evaluated with three seeds and matched
appraisal/linear/shuffled controls; every grammar was trivial (one or two
productions, zero categories).
\end{itemize}

\decision{} Do not spend effort on another identical OmegaSim A6 sweep.
First instrument the inducer so that proposal coverage, accepted moves, data
savings, model-cost increments, and rejection reasons are observable. Then run
an oracle-decomposition battery that separately tests representability,
search, parameter/model coding, and sample complexity. Only after a planted
hierarchical grammar and a fresh chaotic fixture pass complete-code controls
should CLA be used to prioritize OmegaSim regimes.

\section{What CLA currently implements}

The main pipeline is
\[
\begin{aligned}
x_{1:n} &\rightarrow \text{state representation}
\rightarrow \text{symbolizer} \rightarrow s_{1:n}\\
&\rightarrow \text{candidate mining}
\rightarrow \text{greedy grammar induction}\\
&\rightarrow \text{model plus data code}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The \texttt{chaoslang} package began as a dependency-light, pure-Python,
backend-neutral MVP. It contains fixed-grid and fitted microstate
symbolization; n-gram/suffix-trie chunk mining; contextual category proposals;
exact grammar expansion and reconstruction; approximate historical scores;
later complete indexed and compact binary model transmissions; literal,
unigram, Markov-1, Markov-2, and canonical LZ78 controls; frozen-prefix
training; and deterministic experiment artifacts. The backend-neutral fact
layer leaves a seam for a later Hyperon/MeTTa implementation but the reported
measurements are from the Python system.

\begin{longtable}{p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}}
\toprule
Measurement & What it establishes & What it does not establish \\
\midrule
\endhead
Exact reconstruction & The stored grammar/parse expands losslessly to the
symbol stream & Predictive validity, semantic categories, or useful
compression \\
Complete-code win & For the frozen fixture and code, model plus suffix is
shorter than every named complete control & A universal detector or proof of
chaos \\
Shuffled-null rejection & The frozen statistic/code does not prefer the exact
named null & Robustness to every surrogate or confound \\
Held-out n-gram loss & Finite-order predictability of the symbol stream &
Predictive likelihood of the induced CLA grammar \\
Stage-A divergence pass & Local neighbors separate under the frozen geometric
test & A compact grammar, source-law identification, or semantic structure \\
OmegaSim matched-control win & A particular appraisal trace exceeds its
linear and shuffled controls on frozen proxy metrics & Nontrivial grammar,
strange attractor, or causal semantics \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\section{Chronological experiment programme}

\subsection{Baseline symbolic and high-dimensional tests}

Low-dimensional 256-sample tests reconstructed exactly and produced small
grammars. Under the original equal-width rectangular partition, alphabet size
grew rapidly with dimension. The clearest high-dimensional smoke used 1,024
points of 20-dimensional Lorenz--96:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Metric & Value \\
\midrule
Input symbols & 1,024 \\
Unique compound symbols & 510 \\
Accepted rules & 8 \\
Accepted categories & 0 \\
Approximate score & 1,021.2 \\
Fit time & 2.554 s \\
Exact reconstruction & true \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\obs{} Half the positions were unique symbols and the score stayed close to
one proxy unit per input symbol. \inferd{} The primary failure occurred before
grammar induction: a Cartesian product partition makes recurrence sparse in
high dimension. This run cannot imply that Lorenz--96 lacks grammar.
Provenance: experiment
\texttt{20260709T192728Z-lorenz96-1024-dim20-suffix-trie}.

\subsection{R256 lifted Lorenz--63 and representation controls}

A first Phase-1 test lifted Lorenz--63 into 256 dimensions, projected it to a
small kinetic representation, and fitted low-cardinality microstates.
Real-minus-shuffled proxy bits improved from 2.10 under compound M1 to 39.33,
and held-out order-2 n-gram perplexity improved from 433.18 to 3.42. This
showed that the symbolization seam could repair alphabet explosion, but it did
not isolate the benefit of VAMP from clustering.

The preregistered three-seed follow-up evaluated 36 VAMP configurations plus
controls:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Method & Mean proxy margin & Mean n-gram perplexity \\
\midrule
deeptime VAMP (36 rows) & 8.085 & 11.628 \\
direct raw xyz k-means & 12.200 & 3.697 \\
dependency-free reference & 15.167 & 4.151 \\
raw compound M1 & 1.867 & 257.769 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All VAMP and control configurations had positive shuffled-surrogate margins;
no categories emerged. Positive proxy margin was therefore not specific to
VAMP. In the leakage-free rank-conditioned follow-up, selected VAMP beat the
best matched PCA lift on both held-out loss and surrogate margin in 0/3
trajectories at $k=8$ and 0/3 at $k=16$, versus the preregistered requirement
of at least 2/3. VAMP held-out next-symbol loss was worse in every
seed/alphabet stratum. Linear VAMP development was stopped.

\subsection{OmegaSim A6 integration}

The first frozen OmegaSim detector run evaluated 180 rows: five seeds,
couplings 0.35/0.60, delays 0/3, appraisal/linear/shuffled dynamics, and three
feature strata. One of twelve preregistered cells promoted:
coupling 0.60, delay 3, roles8, with four of five matched-seed wins. This was
only bounded proxy evidence.

The subsequent stratified re-sweep completed all 120 planned role-specific A6
cells with three seeds and matched controls. Across the batches, every induced
grammar had one or two productions and zero categories. Individual tuples
occasionally achieved exploratory matched-control wins (including several
4/6, 5/6, and 6/6 cases), but none met the grammar-priority rule of more than
two productions or at least one category.

\obs{} The A6 sweep is complete under its frozen protocol. \inferd{} It does not
show that OmegaSim dynamics are simple; it shows that this CLA
representation/search/coding pipeline does not expose nontrivial grammar in
those traces. Repeating the same pipeline would mainly reproduce the same
instrument limitation.

Provenance:

and batches ending at

\section{Failure decomposition}

\subsection{F1: high-dimensional symbolization}

\textbf{Evidence:} 510 unique symbols in 1,024 points for 20D rectangular
Lorenz--96; raw M1 perplexity around 258 versus 3.7--4.2 for
low-cardinality controls.

\textbf{Status: partially repaired.} Train-fitted k-means/microstates restore
recurrence. Linear VAMP did not outperform matched PCA/raw controls. The next
representation should be justified by a held-out predictive or coding win, not
by kinetic terminology alone.

\subsection{F2: proposal/search coverage}

\textbf{Evidence:} OmegaSim returns nearly invariant one- or two-production,
zero-category grammars; a tiny oracle-like joint chunk/category move can
improve an objective, but ordinary greedy results remain trivial. Synthetic
performance varies sharply across source families.

\hyp{} Greedy single-edit search is myopic: useful categories or hierarchical
rules may require a temporarily costly coordinated move. Candidate mining may
also fail to propose motifs whose occurrences vary in context or phase.

\textbf{Missing diagnostic:} For every iteration, record candidates proposed,
support, data-bit saving, model-bit increment, total delta, overlap conflicts,
and explicit rejection reason. Without this, ``MDL penalty too high'' and
``search never found the grammar'' are observationally confounded.

\subsection{F3: model transmission cost}

\textbf{Evidence:} held-out v1 spent roughly 32.5 kbits per model; indexed and
compact codecs saved hundreds to thousands of bits and reversed some synthetic
decisions, but hard positives still lost because their suffix improvement did
not repay the model. Fibonacci and paperfolding are especially diagnostic:
suffix coding improved substantially while total coding lost.

\hyp{} The current grammar format pays for generality it does not use:
symbol identifiers, rule tables, parse/index metadata, and categories may be
encoded independently rather than through a source-adaptive universal prior.
There may also be duplicated structural information between grammar and parse.

\subsection{F4: data code conditional on the grammar}

\textbf{Evidence:} on attractor fixtures, even 65,536-symbol fixed-model
amortization remained behind Markov-1. This cannot be explained solely by a
one-time model cost. The induced grammar's conditional data code is itself too
weak for these streams.

\hyp{} Exact deterministic expansions are a poor fit to noisy/continuous
dynamics. A probabilistic grammar or state-conditioned residual code may be
needed: grammar for recurrent macrostructure, with a local Markov/arithmetic
coder for deviations.

\subsection{F5: objective mismatch}

The algorithm seeks shortest exact code, while the scientific goal is
compositional, intervention-relevant dynamical structure. LZ78 or Markov codes
can win compression without yielding interpretable grammar, while a useful
semantic grammar may initially cost more bits. Compression remains an
essential anti-overfitting constraint, but one scalar objective may not be an
adequate search guide.

\hyp{} Use a two-stage or multi-objective scheme: first require held-out
predictive gain beyond matched Markov/LZ controls, then select among competitive
models using stability, category recovery, and intervention consistency.

\subsection{F6: sample complexity and nonstationarity}

The synthetic fixed-model source crossed only at 16,384 symbols. This confirms
a real finite-sample threshold. But attractor runs failed even at 65,536
symbols, so ``just use more data'' is not a sufficient general explanation.
Phase sensitivity and changing local partitions suggest that stationary
symbol grammars may be mismatched to nonstationary orbit geometry.

\section{Prioritized algorithm-repair programme}

\subsection*{R0: Build a transparent score decomposition}

Add one machine-readable row per candidate and iteration:
\[
(\text{proposal type},\text{support},\Delta L_{\rm data},
\Delta L_{\rm model},\Delta L_{\rm total},\text{accepted},\text{reason}).
\]
Also emit final totals by symbol dictionary, production topology, parameters,
parse/index stream, and residual/literal escape stream.

\textbf{Acceptance test:} on the generalized complete-code fixture, the report
must reproduce the observed 6,232-bit data saving, 6,296-bit model increment,
and +64-bit rejection exactly.

\subsection*{R1: Oracle representability test}

For each planted family, supply the true generating grammar in the current
internal representation and compute:

\begin{enumerate}
\item oracle grammar plus suffix code;
\item best grammar found by current search;
\item external controls.
\end{enumerate}

If the oracle loses, the representation/code is inadequate. If the oracle wins
but search loses, the problem is mining/search. If both win but learned
categories differ, the problem is identifiability rather than compression.

\subsection*{R2: Search ablations}

On untouched synthetic families, compare:

\begin{itemize}
\item current greedy single move;
\item beam search with widths 4, 16, and 64;
\item paired chunk-plus-category moves;
\item split/merge category moves;
\item grammar initialization from suffix automata or Sequitur/Re-Pair;
\item exact or branch-and-bound search on very small fixtures.
\end{itemize}

Match proposal sets and code accounting. Measure oracle recovery, total bits,
runtime, and the first iteration at which the correct abstraction appears.

\subsection*{R3: Replace deterministic exact grammar with a hybrid code}

Keep exact reconstruction, but encode:

\[
L = L(G) + L(\text{macro parse}\mid G)
    + L(\text{residual symbols}\mid \text{state/context}).
\]

Candidate residual coders should include KT/Dirichlet-smoothed Markov,
context-tree weighting, and arithmetic coding. A grammar move should be
credited only for the incremental conditional saving beyond the same residual
coder without that move. This prevents grammar from being asked to explain
every microscopic fluctuation.

\subsection*{R4: Use hierarchical and probabilistic categories}

Current exact-context categories are brittle. Test soft/overlapping categories
with a transmitted membership model, and hierarchical nonterminals with
explicit reuse. Category success should be measured on planted latent classes:
adjusted mutual information, held-out code gain, stability across seeds, and
counterfactual substitution validity.

\subsection*{R5: Representation contest before grammar induction}

Freeze a common downstream coder and compare:

\begin{itemize}
\item raw scalar/coordinate partitions;
\item delay embeddings;
\item PCA plus k-means;
\item recurrence features;
\item nearest-neighbor local geometry;
\item nonlinear predictive embeddings;
\item OmegaSim task-derived state factors.
\end{itemize}

Fit all transforms on the prefix only. Match alphabet size and occupancy.
Select by held-out code/prediction, not by reconstruction variance. Linear VAMP
should remain retired unless a new hypothesis predicts a specific advantage.

\subsection*{R6: Fresh calibration ladder}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Unit fixtures:} exact grammar recovery on tiny enumeratable
sources.
\item \textbf{Synthetic families:} substitution, hierarchical, stochastic,
phase-shifted, and noisy grammars with exact-marginal nulls.
\item \textbf{Sample-complexity curves:} predeclare horizons and estimate the
crossover distribution across seeds.
\item \textbf{Known dynamics:} fresh Mackey--Glass/Lorenz--96 trajectories,
stable controls, and temporally structured surrogates.
\item \textbf{OmegaSim positive control:} implant a known recoverable grammar
into an A6-like observation channel before testing endogenous dynamics.
\item \textbf{Untouched OmegaSim/A7:} only after the preceding gates pass.
\end{enumerate}

The promotion rule should require every planted positive to beat literal,
unigram, Markov-1/2, context-tree, and LZ78 controls; all nulls must be
rejected; categories must recover planted structure where categories are part
of the fixture.

\section{What not to do}

\begin{itemize}
\item Do not tune thresholds, partitions, horizons, or source parameters on
the already inspected frozen suffixes.
\item Do not treat the nearest-neighbor Stage-A pass as a CLA grammar pass.
\item Do not replace complete-code comparisons with the older compression
proxy.
\item Do not infer ``no chaos'' from a grammar-coding null.
\item Do not enlarge OmegaSim before a planted OmegaSim-like positive control
is recoverable.
\item Do not optimize only model serialization: the 65,536-symbol attractor
failures show that conditional data coding and representation also matter.
\end{itemize}

\section{Bottom line}

\obs{} CLA has crossed from an uncalibrated proxy prototype into a disciplined
experimental system with exact codes, strong provenance, useful negative
results, and one bounded geometric detector pass. It has not crossed the
scientific gate that matters for OmegaSim: a nontrivial induced grammar that
beats strong complete-code controls on fresh chaotic data and remains stable
under matched nulls.

\inferd{} The most likely repair is not one parameter change. The evidence
supports a three-part redesign: transparent oracle-relative search
diagnostics, a more expressive hierarchical/probabilistic grammar with a
competitive residual coder, and representation selection by held-out coding
rather than proxy compression. The proposed D1--D3 experiments can determine
which part is decisive without another broad, ambiguous sweep.
