         From Scheduled Execution to Itinerant Dynamics:
    Three Semantics for Content-Addressable Recurrent Operator
                             Machines
                                            Working note

                                              July 2026


                                              Abstract
          CAROM (Content-Addressable Recurrent Operator Machine) is an architecture that sep-
      arates control from data: opaque command tokens route computation over a small library
      of reusable learned operators, which read, transform, gate, and write a register-like slot
      workspace over a fixed sequence of recurrent steps. This note develops two successive re-
      formulations of its execution model. First, a fixed-point (deep-equilibrium) semantics re-
      places the externally imposed schedule with joint constraint satisfaction: the answer is the
      workspace configuration self-consistent under all commands at once. This buys O(1) training
      memory, adaptive computation, and bidirectional inference, at the cost of the directionality
      that makes composition work. Second, an itinerant semantics recovers directionality without
      reintroducing a clock: commands compete for control of the workspace through a learned
      generalized Lotka–Volterra dynamic, and sequential order emerges as a stable heteroclinic
      channel through metastable states. The scheduled and fixed-point models are exact special
      cases of the itinerant one, obtained respectively by clamping and by symmetrizing a single
      learned inhibition matrix. We give the coupled equations, the stability conditions under
      which the construction is trainable, and a set of discriminating experiments.


1     CAROM: a differentiable register machine
1.1   Intuition
CAROM is best understood as a CPU metaphor made differentiable. A CPU separates three
things that a Transformer entangles: instructions (which computation to perform), registers
(named locations whose contents evolve), and circuits (a small reusable set of functional units
that many instructions share). CAROM reproduces this factorization. Command tokens are
instructions: they never touch data directly, they only select circuitry. Workspace slots are
registers: each has a fixed address, an immutable record of its initial contents, and a mutable
current value. Operators are circuits: a small library of learned read–transform–gate–write
units, shared across all commands, applied to all slots in parallel.
    The bet is that this factorization is the right inductive bias for compositional computation.
If commands can only act through a shared operator library, command semantics are forced to
be combinations of reusable primitives; if slots separate address from value, variable binding
is structural rather than learned. Empirically the architecture performs roughly on par with
a Transformer on generic sequence tasks and shows an advantage when the task is to learn
composable functions over sequences — i.e., the bias pays exactly where it was aimed.

1.3    What is right and what binds
Strengths. Structural variable binding (address/evidence/content decomposition); a learned
reusable operator library; direct interpretability (the routing table A is a readable program dic-
tionary, β t is an execution trace); stable residual gated recurrence; and the empirically observed
compositional advantage.
    The binding constraint is Eq. (3) itself. Three costs follow from the forced schedule. (i)
Sequentiality: T strictly sequential steps, with O(T ) BPTT memory and O(T ) wall-clock depth,

and the same command always routes identically regardless of context. (iii) Fixed budget: T
is a hyperparameter with no halting mechanism. The remainder of this note is about relaxing
Eq. (3) while keeping everything else.


2     The fixed-point reformulation
2.1   Intuition: from procedure to constraint
Scheduled CAROM defines the answer procedurally: wT is whatever executing the commands
in order produces. The fixed-point reformulation defines it declaratively: the answer is the
workspace configuration that is self-consistent under all commands simultaneously. Each com-
mand stops being an instruction executed at a moment and becomes a standing constraint —
“slot contents should relate thus” — and inference is relaxation to the configuration on which
all constraints agree. For a content-addressable workspace this is arguably the more natural
semantics: the workspace settles into the interpretation jointly licensed by the commands. It is
the denotational semantics of the same program whose operational semantics is Eq. (3).

2.3     Why this improves scheduled CAROM
 (a) Parallelism and memory. Each solver sweep updates all slots under all commands simul-
     taneously; sequential depth becomes “iterations to convergence,” a property of problem
     hardness rather than program length, and training memory is O(1) by Eq. (6).

 (b) Adaptive computation for free. Iterate to a residual tolerance: easy inputs converge fast,
     hard inputs take more sweeps, and test-time compute scales cleanly — more iterations
     buy a better answer to the same well-defined problem.

    (c) Bidirectional inference. A late constraint under pressure propagates residual back and
        revises earlier slots. Scheduled execution structurally forbids this; parsing-like, abductive,
        and error-correcting tasks require it.

 (d) A confidence channel. Per-slot residual magnitude at budget reports which parts of the
     constraint system failed to settle.

    Note also that Jacobi iteration of F from w0 = e simulates forward execution: in sweep one,
only constraints grounded in raw evidence act; in sweep two, constraints depending on those
results activate; and so on, so information traverses the dependency DAG in critical-path-many
sweeps.

2.4     What it loses
Composition is directional: in f (g(x)), g’s result must inform f and not conversely. The schedule
provided this by temporal order; the fixed point must encode it through the learned asymmetries
induced by the step embeddings sm , and whether learned asymmetry suffices is an open empirical
question. More fundamentally, a single global equilibrium has no internal phases: it cannot
represent “first establish X, then, in the context of X, establish Y ” except by folding both into
one simultaneous constraint set. This motivates the next section.


3      Itinerant dynamics: order without a clock
3.1     Intuition
The scheduled model imposes order from outside; the fixed-point model deletes it. The itinerant
model lets order emerge. The device is to promote the schedule from an input to a dynamical
variable: introduce a low- dimensional control state in which commands compete for control of
the workspace. The winning command’s operators push the workspace toward local consistency
(a phase of constraint satisfaction); the changed workspace alters the competitive fitness of the
other commands; a successor takes over. Computation becomes a trajectory through a chain
of metastable way-stations — linger, work, hand off — and the visitation order is carved into
a learned inhibition structure rather than clocked in. Because a command’s fitness reads the
workspace, the itinerary is data-dependent: conditionals, loops, and halting arise as dynamical


                                                   4
phenomena. Within each phase one has equilibrium semantics (bidirectional constraint propa-
gation); across phases, directional composition. The two semantics of Sections 1–2 coexist at
different timescales of a single flow, and each is recovered exactly as a special case.
    The relevant dynamical object is the stable heteroclinic channel (Afraimovich–Rabinovich):
a chain of saddle equilibria connected by heteroclinic orbits which is itself an attracting set.
Unlike a strange attractor, an SHC has no positive Lyapunov exponents — trajectories contract
transversally into the channel — so the construction stays on trainable ground while occupying
far wider computational territory than a fixed point.

Trajectories are funneled into the channel, dwell near each saddle, and pass onward in the

fatigue guarantees no station becomes a trap.

Halting and readout. The halt mode has learned growth rate σhalt (w) — a “done” detector
on the workspace — and is made absorbing (ρhalt,j large for all j, nothing inhibits it strongly).
Readout fires on w when ahalt crosses threshold: variable-length computation with learned
halting and no auxiliary halting machinery. A second output channel comes free: thresholding
a(t) yields the itinerary — the sequence of saddles visited with dwell times — an emergent, data-
dependent program trace, more informative than the scheduled model’s trace precisely because
it was chosen rather than copied from the input.

3.3   Timescale separation and training
Take the workspace fast and the control slow (λ large relative to the scale of Eq. (7)). Then within
each metastable phase, w approximately equilibrates under the currently dominant mixture:
each phase is a small deep-equilibrium solve, differentiable by Eq. (6) at O(1) memory. The
control trajectory a(t) is low-dimensional (M ≪ nd) and is backpropagated directly — pathwise
gradients through the reparameterized noise — at negligible cost. Training is thus a chain of
implicit solves stitched by a cheap explicit trajectory: no BPTT through the workspace history.
Because the SHC has no positive Lyapunov exponents, the exploding and ill-defined gradients
of chaotic regimes do not arise; the sensitive quantities are saddle passage times (logarithmic
in noise, hence soft) and the discrete itinerary near decision boundaries of σm (w), handled by
temperature on the σ readouts annealed toward sharpness.

3.4   Why itinerancy improves on the fixed point
 (a) Directionality restored, structurally. Phase order is a property of the channel, not a learned
     feature hint: “first X, then Y in the context of X” is representable as two saddles, which
     no single global equilibrium expresses.
 (b) Data-dependent control flow. Since σm reads w, a command invades only when its pre-
     condition pattern is present — content-triggered dispatch, which command-identity-only
     routing forbids — and cycles in ρ with workspace-dependent exit give genuine loops.
 (c) Both semantics at once. Within a phase: equilibrium relaxation, bidirectional constraint
     propagation, error correction. Across phases: directional composition. The fixed point
     offers only the former; the schedule only the latter.

 (d) Learned halting and emergent traces, as above; neither exists in the fixed-point model
     except through solver tolerance.

    (e) Strict containment. By Proposition 1, nothing is lost: the model can collapse to either pre-
        decessor where a task rewards it, and which collapse a task induces is itself an experimental
        observable.

3.5     Failure modes and regularizers
Channel collapse (a saddle becomes a sink; computation stalls): guaranteed against by fatigue,
or monitored via saddle eigenvalues. Channel evaporation (νm < 1 somewhere; trajectories leak):
regularize Eq. (10) with a margin along realized itineraries. Phase smearing (a stays diffuse;
the soft-mixture blur returns): entropy penalty on a encouraging near-vertex dwell. Itinerary
instability (noise resamples yield different routes on the same input): penalize itinerary variance
across resamples on deterministic tasks — while noting that on genuinely ambiguous inputs
route variance is the model sampling interpretations, a feature connected to stochastic- attractor
readouts rather than a defect.


4      Experiments
Throughout, evaluate where the inductive bias lives: compositional splits, program execution,
length and depth generalization — not perplexity.
Experiment 1 (Localizing the value of recurrence). Unroll scheduled CAROM into L feed-
forward layers sharing the operator library (schedule becomes layer assignment) and compare
on the compositional tasks where CAROM beat the Transformer. If the advantage survives
unrolling, the command/operator factorization and register workspace carry it and the cheap
parallel version ships; if not, recurrence is load-bearing and the reformulations of Sections 2–3
are the right investment.
Experiment 2 (Forward composition vs. backward revision). Construct function-composition
tasks in two regimes: (i) pure forward chains, and (ii) chains containing an embedded inverse
problem, solvable only by propagating a late constraint back to revise earlier slots. Predictions:
scheduled CAROM matches the fixed-point model on (i), with the fixed-point model’s sweep
count tracking critical-path depth (measurable and falsifiable); the fixed-point model pulls ahead
on (ii). Failure of the fixed-point model on (i) indicates that step-embedding asymmetry is
insufficient and motivates causal masking of the constraint graph.
Experiment 3 (Emergent order). Present composed functions with commands in scrambled
order, so correct execution order must be discovered from data. Scheduled CAROM is struc-
turally handicapped; the fixed-point model must absorb order into the equilibrium; the itinerant
model can express order but must learn it. Success criterion: extracted itineraries (thresholded
a(t)) match the ground-truth dependency order on held-out compositions. This is the sharpest
test of the central claim — that a network’s trajectory through state space can be a readable,
data-dependent program.
Experiment 4 (Loops and halting). Tasks with data-dependent iteration counts (e.g., iterate
a map until a predicate holds; string rewriting to normal form). Measure: halt-mode timing ac-
curacy; generalization to iteration counts beyond the training range, where scheduled CAROM

                                                  7
cannot even represent the larger programs; dwell-time statistics against the ln(1/ηa )/λu predic-
tion.

Experiment 5 (Which semantics does the task induce?). Train the itinerant model with ρ
unregularized on (i) constraint- satisfaction tasks and (ii) sequential-algorithmic tasks; track the
asymmetry ∥ρ−ρ⊤ ∥ and the Lyapunov spectrum of the realized dynamics. Prediction: (i) drives
ρ toward symmetry (equilibrium semantics rediscovered), (ii) toward chain asymmetry (proce-
dural semantics rediscovered). Observing task-dependent selection of computational semantics
within one architecture would be a more interesting result than either endpoint alone.

Experiment 6 (Ablations on the itinerancy mechanism). (a) Fatigue only vs. asymmetric
inhibition only vs. both, testing the claim that asymmetry proposes order while fatigue prevents
traps. (b) Noise amplitude sweeps, testing dwell-time control and the deterministic limit. (c) σm
made workspace-independent, ablating data-dependent dispatch: conditionals and loops should
fail selectively.


5    Closing remark
The three models are one architecture under three execution semantics — operational (Sec-
tion 1), denotational (Section 2), and itinerant (Section 3) — sharing a single operator library
and routing table. The itinerant formulation contains the other two as limits of one learned ma-
trix, and adds what both lack: emergent, data-dependent, interpretable control flow, realized as
stable heteroclinic itinerancy rather than either an external clock or a single global equilibrium.
Metastable attractor ruins as the phases of a computation, transitions as inference steps, the
itinerary as the program: this is attractor dynamics as cognition, in a form that is trainable
with current machinery.
