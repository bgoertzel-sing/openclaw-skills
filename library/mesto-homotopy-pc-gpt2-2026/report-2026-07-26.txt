      Homotopy Distillation of a Transformer into a
             Predictive-Coding Network
         Faithful re-expression of GPT-2 (124M) with local, error-based learning
                                  Technical report, work in progress

                                                Ahmad Mesto
                       UNSW Sydney          ·    a.mesto@student.unsw.edu.au

                                                  Abstract
      Predictive coding (PC) is a biologically motivated alternative to backpropagation in which
      learning is driven by local prediction errors rather than a global backward pass. A standing
      obstacle is scale: PC is rarely demonstrated beyond small networks, and openly documented
      PC transformers remain at the order of 105 parameters. We re-express a full-scale trans-
      former, GPT-2 at 124M parameters, three orders of magnitude beyond that scale, as a
      predictive-coding network by homotopy distillation: a pretrained transformer is continu-
      ously deformed into an error-parameterised PC network along a schedule τ = η T , from a
      backpropagation anchor at T =1 to a fully relaxed PC regime at terminal τ =6.4. Three re-
      sults are established to date. (i) The distillation is near-lossless and reproducible: across five
      independent runs the student’s next-token distribution tracks the teacher to a prompt KL
      of ∼ 10−5 nats at every milestone, nearly four thousand times inside the divergence-abort
      threshold, with held-out perplexity within 0.4%; both are ε=0 feedforward evaluations with
      no teacher present. A completed 5 × 107 -token production run on an OpenWebText shard
      extends this by an order of magnitude, worst milestone KL 3.1 × 10−5 . (ii) Along the ho-
      motopy the learning signal deforms as designed: the per-block PC weight update, probed
      teacher-free against plain backpropagation, grows from the anchor identity η at T =1 to full
      backpropagation magnitude, its direction staying aligned at a per-block cosine above 0.998
      throughout. (iii) At 124M the PC error field is strongly concentrated: the top 5% of error
      cells carry ≈ 90% of the squared update mass, the frontier sits in early and middle blocks
      rather than late ones, and both properties survive a teacher-free cross-entropy settle. This
      concentration is the lever for compressing the settle, the dominant cost of PC at scale. In
      companion work the same transformer’s full forward pass runs natively inside the MORK
      symbolic knowledge store, matched to the reference implementation to 6 × 10−6 relative er-
      ror; predictive coding is the learning mode designed for that substrate, and the two results
      are halves of one programme. We close with the reproducibility methodology, an explicit
      statement of what is not yet shown, and the planned experiments.


1 Motivation and scope
Predictive coding learns by settling a network of state and error units to a low-energy configura-
tion and then updating weights from purely local error signals; under standard assumptions its
updates approximate those of backpropagation on arbitrary computation graphs [1, 2, 3]. This
locality is attractive for two properties backpropagation does not offer: learning can proceed
online and in place without a global backward pass, and the resulting dynamics are a candidate
substrate for continual learning without catastrophic forgetting. The obstacle has always been
scale. Public demonstrations of PC on transformer-shaped models remain at toy sizes (order
105 parameters), and it is an open question whether the PC parameterisation is even expressive
enough to hold a modern transformer at realistic width and depth.
    This work attacks that question directly, but by a deliberately conservative route. Rather
than training a transformer from scratch under PC, which is compute-prohibitive for reasons dis-
cussed in §5, we ask whether an already-trained transformer can be re-expressed as a predictive-
coding network without loss of function, at 124M parameters. A positive answer establishes
expressivity at scale and yields the substrate on which the continual-learning properties that
motivate PC can then be tested.

Contribution, stated precisely. This report claims: (a) a method, homotopy distillation,
that deforms a pretrained transformer into an error-parameterised PC network; (b) an aﬀirma-
tive answer, at 124M, to the expressivity question: the PC parameterisation holds a modern
transformer, near-losslessly and reproducibly, with local updates; and (c) a measurement of the
PC error-mass distribution not previously available at this scale. It does not claim that PC
trains a transformer from scratch, nor that PC outperforms backpropagation; those are sepa-
rate questions, one of which (the continual-learning advantage) is a designed but not-yet-run
experiment described in §7.

2 Method
The teacher is a frozen, pretrained GPT-2 (124M) [6]. The student is initialised from the teacher
and trained under an error-based (ePC-style) parameterisation in which the prediction errors
are the only free leaves and weight updates are computed locally from them [4, 5]. Training
minimises a temperature-2 knowledge-distillation KL to the teacher.

2.2 The homotopy schedule
The relaxation depth T follows a geometric schedule T ∈ {1, 2, 4, . . . }, and the effective relax-
ation time is τ = η T with step size η fixed within a run; the schedule runs from the T =1 anchor
to terminal τ =6.4, a fully relaxed PC regime: eight rungs at η = 0.05, seven at η = 0.10. A
stage advances only when an energy-convergence hold criterion passes, and its τ step subdi-
vides when it does not. Increasing T deforms the network continuously from backpropagation
toward PC equilibrium, so that at no point does training leave a regime in which the student
is a faithful copy of the teacher. Monitoring uses scale-invariant and rank statistics throughout
(an energy-convergence ratio and trailing-median baselines) rather than absolute thresholds, for
reasons that §4 makes concrete.
    Each run of the replicate family processes 5.0 × 106 tokens at sequence length 512. The
5.0×107 -token production run has since completed; its fidelity is reported alongside the replicate
results below.

3 Results established to date
3.1 The re-expression is near-lossless and reproducible
Figure 1 shows the student–teacher prompt KL across the anneal for five independent runs (the
development run plus a four-cell replicate matrix spanning two random seeds and two micro-
batch shapes). The worst milestone KL across all five runs is 1.3 × 10−5 nats, roughly 3.9 × 103
times inside the divergence-abort threshold, and held-out perplexity stays within 0.4% of the
teacher throughout. Both quantities are evaluated on the student’s plain feedforward pass, ε = 0
with no settle, and the teacher is absent from the evaluation: these are deployment-condition
numbers. The teacher enters training only through the settle objective L of §2. The divergence-
abort guard never fired in any run, so no run was excluded from this set. The fidelity holds
at every stage of the deformation, not only at its endpoint, and reproduces across seeds and
batch shapes. This answers the expressivity question of §1 in the aﬀirmative at 124M: the PC
parameterisation holds a modern transformer.
    The result now extends by an order of magnitude. The 5.0 × 107 -token production run
trains at error step η = 0.10 with micro-batch 10 in fp32, a configuration first validated by its
own replicate cell at pilot scale, over a seven-rung schedule to the same terminal τ = 6.4; the
training data is a pre-tokenised OpenWebText shard, and the run completed in 35.7 hours on a
single A100. Fidelity holds at ten times the tokens: the worst milestone KL across its fifty-one
milestones is 3.1×10−5 nats, sixteen hundred times inside the abort threshold, the final milestone
sits at 5.8 × 10−6 , and held-out perplexity stays within 0.58% of the teacher throughout, the
terminal deviation in the student’s favour. The run required no energy-convergence holds and
no stage subdivisions end to end; its gradient-space path is taken up in §3.2.

3.2 The learning signal grows while its direction is held
Fidelity alone could be trivial if the relaxation never left the backpropagation anchor. Figure 2
shows what the homotopy does to the learning signal, measured teacher-free: at every milestone
a probe computes, per block, the weight gradient of the settled local objective (3) and the weight
gradient of plain backpropagation, both under a cross-entropy loss on data. At the anchor the
norm ratio between the two is 0.05, equal to η, exactly as the T =1 identity of §2.1 predicts.
By terminal τ the ratio reaches 0.98, full backpropagation magnitude, while the per-τ gain,
the ratio divided by τ , falls from 1 to 0.15: the settle is genuinely deep, no longer a scaled
single step. Through the whole deformation the minimum per-block cosine between the PC and
backpropagation gradients never falls below 0.998. The learning signal leaves the anchor regime
in magnitude while never leaving backpropagation’s direction at any depth; this is the PC-
approximates-backprop correspondence [2] observed block-resolved at 124M along a continuous
path. The 5 × 107 production run retraces this path at doubled error step: the ratio opens at

3.3 The error field is strongly concentrated at scale
Figure 3 reports a measurement, to our knowledge not previously available at this scale, of
how PC update mass is distributed across the 4.72 × 106 error cells of the terminal settle. The
distribution is strongly concentrated: ranked by magnitude, the top 5% of cells carry ≈ 90%
of the squared update mass. The concentration is rank-form, and it depends on no tail model:
absolute magnitude thresholds do not separate a frontier, since nearly all cells sit below any fixed
cutoff, yet ranked by magnitude the mass is extremely concentrated. The rank concentration
has a direct engineering consequence for the settle cost discussed in §5: a settle that updates
only the top-ranked cells could recover most of the update at a fraction of the work.
    As a τ -comparable summary of tail weight we also record a Hill statistic at a fixed depth
of the top 5%, k = 235,929 of the 4.72M cells: α = 1.681 at τ =6.4; the sampling standard
error of this fixed-depth statistic, estimated model-free from the empirical dispersion of the
log-excesses in the stored histogram, is ≈ 0.002. Across τ the statistic moves by at most 0.008
(1.673, 1.679, 1.681 at τ = 1.0, 3.2, 6.4), a few sampling errors, so tail shape at fixed depth is
nearly τ -invariant. We do not claim a limiting power law: sweeping the tail depth from the top
0.5% to the top 20% moves the Hill statistic monotonically from ≈ 2.2 to ≈ 1.1 with no plateau,
several hundred sampling errors of movement (sweep from the same histogram), so α serves as
a fixed-depth descriptor of tail weight rather than an estimate of a tail exponent.
    Two further questions about this structure matter: where the frontier lives in depth, and
whether it needs the teacher. Both now have measured answers. Depth first. The settle
activity is spread across the network rather than piled into late blocks: at terminal τ the final
block carries the smallest share of the last relaxation step’s update norm, 0.4%. The end-state
frontier tells the same story more sharply. Decomposing the top-5% frontier by block, on an

4 Reproducibility methodology
Acceptance is governed by a pre-registered battery of six criteria (a knowledge-distillation an-
chor, per-milestone prompt KL, held-out perplexity delta, terminal block cosine, an error-norm-
ratio monotonicity condition, and an energy-convergence hold count). During the replicate cam-
paign a methodological issue surfaced that shapes how the planned comparisons are designed.
The initial KL and cosine thresholds had been frozen from a single pilot run; the four-cell repli-
cate matrix then showed that these thresholds sat inside the run-to-run reproducibility noise: a
nominal-configuration replicate drifted further from the pilot than a compute-lever variant did.
The bars were therefore recalibrated, under a rule fixed before the last replicates were seen, to
the strict envelope over the replicate set (n ≥ 3); and configuration comparisons are henceforth
run at matched seeds and judged on per-seed deltas, which cancels seed variance. The lesson is
general and is carried into the experiments below: never set an absolute acceptance threshold
from a single sample of a chaotic training signal.

   • Re-expression, not from-scratch training. The teacher did the original learning;
     the contribution is that PC can hold the result at 124M with local updates. Whether
     PC can train a transformer of this size from random initialisation, competitively with
     backpropagation, is open. The blocker is compute: each PC weight update requires an
     inner relaxation of T forward/backward passes where backpropagation requires one, so
     from-scratch PC at scale is one to two orders of magnitude more expensive per update.
     The concentration measurement of §3.3 is a first lever on exactly this cost.
   • Token scale. The replicate family uses 5 × 106 tokens and the completed production run
     5 × 107 ; both remain far short of pretraining scale.
   • The motivating advantage is not yet demonstrated. That the PC form forgets
     less or transfers better than a backpropagation baseline, which is the reason to prefer PC
     at all, is a designed comparison that has not been run [planned]. Nothing in this report
     should be read as evidence for it.

6 Companion result: the same transformer inside a symbolic store
The PC question of this report is asked because of a companion result by the author, previously
unpublished, which supplies the substrate. The full GPT-2 (124M) forward pass runs natively
inside MORK, the MeTTa Optimal Reduction Kernel of the OpenCog Hyperon programme [7,
8]: weights and activations live as expressions in the kernel’s trie-backed space, and the forward
pass executes as the kernel’s own rule application over tensor-block expressions, with dense
numeric kernels (einsum, attention, normalisation) added by the author as sink operations.
The in-store forward matches the reference implementation to a maximum relative logit error
of 6.06 × 10−6 with argmax agreement, and a blocked tensor format brings the 12-layer forward
from 124.5M store expressions to 7.8M, a 16× reduction, at the identical maximum relative
error.

                                                                                              6
    Decoding is incremental and exact. Causal masking means old columns never read new ones,
and einsum is bilinear, so appending one token produces exactly a one-column delta through
all twelve layers; this is the KV-cache identity derived as incremental view maintenance rather
than assumed as a heuristic. A 32-token greedy decode runs in a single kernel invocation, with
all 32 tokens equal to the reference oracle.
    The two results are halves of one programme. A store-hosted transformer makes weights,
activations, and symbolic knowledge one queryable, incrementally maintained structure; what
such a substrate cannot do cheaply is a global, synchronised backward pass. Predictive coding
is the learning mode that fits it: the updates of §2 are local to each block, and by §3.3 the
update mass concentrates on a small ranked frontier of cells, exactly the sparse, incremental
write pattern a trie-backed store maintains well. This report establishes the learning-side half
at full model scale; the companion result establishes the inference-side half.

7 Planned work
Three lines follow directly. (1) A continual-learning benchmark: sequential-domain training
measuring forgetting and transfer for the PC form against backpropagation fine-tuning at
matched compute, with the matched-seed, envelope design of §4. (2) A coupled-objective grid
separating whether any advantage comes from the PC parameterisation or from objective cou-
pling. (3) Path-faithful settle compression (higher-order integrators at fixed evaluation budget
and a rank-frontier settle informed by the measured rank concentration), the enabling technol-
ogy for pushing toward from-scratch PC training at reduced scale. Results from (1) and (2)
are the immediate next milestone. Longer term, the target is the substrate of §6: predictive
coding as the learning rule of the store-native transformer, with frontier-compressed settles as
the bridge. Figure 4 places the report in this programme.
