      Homotopy Distillation of a Transformer into a
             Predictive-Coding Network
         Faithful re-expression of GPT-2 (124M) with local, error-based learning
                                 Technical report, work in progress

                                               Ahmad Mesto

                                                 Abstract
     Predictive coding (PC) is a biologically motivated alternative to backpropagation in which
     learning is driven by local prediction errors rather than a global backward pass. A standing
     obstacle is scale: PC is rarely demonstrated beyond small networks, and openly documented
     PC transformers remain at the order of 105 parameters. We re-express a full-scale trans-
     former, GPT-2 at 124M parameters, three orders of magnitude beyond that scale, as a
     predictive-coding network by homotopy distillation: a pretrained transformer is continu-
     ously deformed into an error-parameterised PC network along a schedule τ = η T , from a
     backpropagation anchor at T =1 to a fully relaxed PC regime at terminal τ =6.4. Three re-
     sults are established to date. (i) The distillation is near-lossless and reproducible: across five
     independent runs the student’s next-token distribution tracks the teacher to a prompt KL
     of ∼ 10−5 nats at every milestone, nearly four thousand times inside the divergence-abort
     threshold, with held-out perplexity within 0.4%. (ii) Along the homotopy the internal rep-
     resentation transforms substantially, the mean per-block error-norm ratio rising 0.05 → 0.98,
     while functional equivalence to the teacher is preserved at a minimum per-block cosine above
     0.998. (iii) At 124M the PC error field is strongly concentrated: the top 5% of error cells
     carry ≈ 90% of the squared update mass, with a fixed-depth Hill statistic α ≈ 1.68 at the
     top-5% tail that is stable across τ . This concentration is the lever for compressing the settle,
     the dominant cost of PC at scale. In companion work the same transformer’s full forward
     pass runs natively inside the MORK symbolic knowledge store, matched to the reference
     implementation to 6 × 10−6 relative error; predictive coding is the learning mode designed
     for that substrate, and the two results are halves of one program. We close with the repro-
     ducibility methodology, an explicit statement of what is not yet shown, and the planned
     experiments.


1 Motivation and scope
Predictive coding learns by settling a network of state and error units to a low-energy configura-
tion and then updating weights from purely local error signals; under standard assumptions its
updates approximate those of backpropagation on arbitrary computation graphs [1, 2, 3]. This
locality is attractive for two reasons that backpropagation does not offer: learning can proceed
online and in place without a global backward pass, and the resulting dynamics are a natural
substrate for continual learning without catastrophic forgetting. The obstacle has always been
scale. Public demonstrations of PC on transformer-shaped models remain at toy sizes (order
105 parameters), and it is an open question whether the PC parameterisation is even expressive
enough to hold a modern transformer at realistic width and depth.
    This work attacks that question directly, but by a deliberately conservative route. Rather
than training a transformer from scratch under PC, which is compute-prohibitive for reasons dis-
cussed in §5, we ask whether an already-trained transformer can be re-expressed as a predictive-
coding network without loss of function, at 124M parameters. A positive answer establishes
expressivity at scale and yields the substrate on which the continual-learning properties that
motivate PC can then be tested.
Contribution, stated precisely. This report claims: (a) a method, homotopy distillation,
that deforms a pretrained transformer into an error-parameterised PC network; (b) an aﬀirma-

transformer, near-losslessly and reproducibly, with local updates; and (c) a measurement of the
PC error-mass distribution not previously available at this scale. It does not claim that PC
trains a transformer from scratch, nor that PC outperforms backpropagation; those are sepa-

experiment described in §7.

2 Method
The teacher is a frozen, pretrained GPT-2 (124M) [6]. The student is initialised from the teacher
and trained under an error-based (ePC-style) parameterisation in which the prediction errors
are the only free leaves and weight updates are computed locally from them [4, 5]. Distillation
minimises a temperature-2 knowledge-distillation KL to the teacher.

2.2 The homotopy schedule
The relaxation depth T follows a geometric schedule T ∈ {1, 2, 4, . . . , 128}, and the effective
relaxation time is τ = η T with step size η fixed within a run; the schedule runs from the
T =1 anchor to terminal τ =6.4, a fully relaxed PC regime. A stage advances only when an
energy-convergence hold criterion passes, and its τ step subdivides when it does not. Increasing
T deforms the network continuously from backpropagation toward PC equilibrium, so that

3 Results established to date
3.1 The re-expression is near-lossless and reproducible
Figure 1 shows the student–teacher prompt KL across the anneal for five independent runs (the
development run plus a four-cell replicate matrix spanning two random seeds and two micro-
batch shapes). The worst milestone KL across all five runs is 1.3 × 10−5 nats, roughly 3.9 × 103
times inside the divergence-abort threshold, and held-out perplexity stays within 0.4% of the
teacher throughout. The divergence-abort guard never fired in any run, so no run was excluded
from this set. The fidelity holds at every stage of the deformation, not only at its endpoint, and
reproduces across seeds and batch shapes. This answers the expressivity question of §1 in the
aﬀirmative at 124M: the PC parameterisation holds a modern transformer.

3.2 The representation moves while the function is held
Fidelity alone could be trivial if nothing changed internally. Figure 2 shows it does not: as
τ increases from 0.05 to 6.4, the mean per-block error-norm ratio rises from 0.05 to 0.98, a
substantial reorganisation of the internal representation toward the PC form, while the minimum
per-block cosine to the teacher never falls below 0.998. The student travels a long way in
representation space and remains functionally equivalent the whole way. This is the property
the homotopy is designed to produce.

nearly τ -invariant. We do not claim a limiting power law: sweeping the tail depth from the top
0.5% to the top 20% moves the Hill statistic monotonically from ≈ 2.2 to ≈ 1.1 with no plateau,
several hundred sampling errors of movement (sweep from the same histogram), so α serves as
a fixed-depth descriptor of tail weight rather than an estimate of a tail exponent.

4 Reproducibility methodology
Acceptance is governed by a pre-registered battery of six criteria (a knowledge-distillation an-
chor, per-milestone prompt KL, held-out perplexity delta, terminal block cosine, an error-norm-
ratio monotonicity condition, and an energy-convergence hold count). During the replicate cam-
paign a methodological issue surfaced that shapes how the planned comparisons are designed.
The initial KL and cosine thresholds had been frozen from a single pilot run; the four-cell repli-
cate matrix then showed that these thresholds sat inside the run-to-run reproducibility noise: a
nominal-configuration replicate drifted further from the pilot than a compute-lever variant did.
The bars were therefore recalibrated, under a rule fixed before the last replicates were seen, to
the strict envelope over the replicate set (n ≥ 3); and configuration comparisons are henceforth
run at matched seeds and judged on per-seed deltas, which cancels seed variance. The lesson is
general and is carried into the experiments below: never set an absolute acceptance threshold
from a single sample of a chaotic training signal.

5 Status and limitations
Three things are established: the PC parameterisation holds a modern transformer at 124M,
the deformation reorganises the representation while preserving function, and the error field
carries an exploitable rank structure. This is a progress report, so the boundaries are stated
with the same force.

   • Re-expression, not from-scratch training. The teacher did the original learning;
     the contribution is that PC can hold the result at 124M with local updates. Whether
     PC can train a transformer of this size from random initialisation, competitively with
     backpropagation, is open. The blocker is compute: each PC weight update requires an
     inner relaxation of T forward/backward passes where backpropagation requires one, so
     from-scratch PC at scale is one to two orders of magnitude more expensive per update.
     The concentration measurement of §3.3 is a first lever on exactly this cost.
   • Token scale. The runs here use 5 × 106 tokens; a 5 × 107 run is in progress [running].
   • The motivating advantage is not yet demonstrated. That the PC form forgets
     less or transfers better than a backpropagation baseline, which is the reason to prefer PC
     at all, is a designed comparison that has not been run [planned]. Nothing in this report
     should be read as evidence for it.

6 Companion result: the same transformer inside a symbolic store
The PC question of this report is asked because of a companion result by the author, previously
unpublished, which supplies the substrate. The full GPT-2 (124M) forward pass runs natively
inside MORK, the MeTTa Optimal Reduction Kernel of the OpenCog Hyperon program [7, 8]:
weights and activations live as expressions in the kernel’s trie-backed space, and the forward pass
executes as the kernel’s own rule application over tensor-block expressions, with dense numeric
kernels (einsum, attention, normalisation) added by the author as sink operations. The in-store
forward matches the reference implementation to a maximum relative logit error of 6.06 × 10−6

with argmax agreement, and a blocked tensor format brings the 12-layer forward from 124.5M
store expressions to 7.8M, a 16× reduction, at the identical maximum relative error.
    Decoding is incremental and exact. Causal masking means old columns never read new ones,
and einsum is bilinear, so appending one token produces exactly a one-column delta through
all twelve layers; this is the KV-cache identity derived as incremental view maintenance rather
than assumed as a heuristic. A 32-token greedy decode runs in a single kernel invocation, with
all 32 tokens equal to the reference oracle.
    The two results are halves of one program. A store-hosted transformer makes weights,
activations, and symbolic knowledge one queryable, incrementally maintained structure; what
such a substrate cannot do cheaply is a global, synchronised backward pass. Predictive coding
is the learning mode that fits it: the updates of §2 are local to each block, and by §3.3 the
update mass concentrates on a small ranked frontier of cells, exactly the sparse incremental
writes a trie-backed store maintains well. This report establishes the learning-side half at full
model scale; the companion result establishes the inference-side half.

7 Planned work
Three lines follow directly. (1) A continual-learning benchmark: sequential-domain training
measuring forgetting and transfer for the PC form against backpropagation fine-tuning at
matched compute, with the matched-seed, envelope design of §4. (2) A coupled-objective grid
separating whether any advantage comes from the PC parameterisation or from objective cou-
pling. (3) Path-faithful settle compression (higher-order integrators at fixed evaluation budget
and a rank-frontier settle informed by the measured rank concentration), the enabling technol-
ogy for pushing toward from-scratch PC training at reduced scale. Results from (1) and (2)
are the immediate next milestone. Longer term, the target is the substrate of §6: predictive
coding as the learning rule of the store-native transformer, with frontier-compressed settles as
the bridge. Figure 4 places the report in this programme.
