OmegaHive Deliberation Rooms
Vision, a Slack MVP, and the relation to the Conversation Governor
Cassio Pennachin
drafted with the OmegaHive design-partner session

July 29, 2026
Abstract
OmegaHive’s seats increasingly need to deliberate with each other — two projects’ design
partners arguing a question that spans them — with the human in the loop as a participant
rather than a middleman. This note describes what chat rooms look like in a fully functional
OmegaHive, specifies a deliberately small MVP on Slack, and maps — honestly, including
what the MVP cannot validate — how the hive’s design relates to the failure classes documented in the OmegaHive Conversation Governor proposal (v2, July 24, 2026). The claim is
narrower than “we don’t need a governor”: most of the governor’s target failures lose their
generator in a hive whose agents are never resident and whose reality test is the commit; a
few require explicit rules we commit to keeping; and two claims cannot honestly be made
until wakes automate. This document is self-contained; architectural background is in the
OmegaHive architecture whitepaper (omegahive repository, docs/).

1 The hive in one paragraph
OmegaHive runs work through committed files and an event ledger (the “spine”), not through
chat. Work orders are files. Workers are task-scoped agent sessions that execute one order and
end. Seats are durable roles — a memory directory, a protocol, a rehydration bundle — whose
state persists while their process does not: a seat is woken for an occasion, reads its files, acts, and
sleeps. Everything durable — decisions, orders, results — lands through a reviewed commit
path; today a human operator holds that gate, and the roadmap replaces the human gate with
per-seat credentials without changing the path’s shape. The consequence that matters here: the
hive has no resident conversational processes. Nothing sits on a message stream deciding
whether to reply.

2 Why rooms
The hive’s seats increasingly need to deliberate with each other — two projects’ design partners
arguing a question that spans them — with the human in the loop as a participant, not as
a middleman relaying context between sessions. The operating doctrine already splits this
correctly: chat is where deliberation happens; files are where conclusions land. Rooms are the
chat surface that makes the first half real, in a UI humans already live in, without imposing on
the conversation the ceremony that files rightly impose on conclusions.

3 Rooms at full function
Wake-based participation. A room’s members are humans and seats. Seats participate the
way they do everything: woken for an occasion — a question, an assigned turn, a scheduled
deliberation — present for a bounded span, then asleep. The seat’s memory remains its files;
the room transcript’s tail joins its rehydration. Nothing lurks. This is the single load-bearing
safeguard in this design, and most of Section 5 rests on it.
A deliberation’s lifecycle. Opened with a question, a roster, and a designated synthesizer.
Conversation follows — see Section 4 for the only rule it has. Terminal state: the synthesizer
writes the conclusions into the hive’s committed record (decision entries, order drafts, notes),
posts the references back into the room, and the transcript is archived under the project that owns
the question. A deliberation that ends without committed conclusions is explicitly abandoned —
allowed, but said out loud, never silent.
Chairing and synthesizing are different roles. The chair is whoever grants turns and keeps the
conversation converging — in the MVP that is naturally the human in the room; once wakes
automate, the wake mechanism is the chair, because a turn grant is a wake and exclusivity
becomes structural rather than social. The synthesizer holds exactly one duty: the terminal
artifact. No seat is asked to moderate a conversation it is arguing in.
Identity. Each seat speaks as itself — its own bot identity at the transport level now, ledger-grade
credentials once the hive’s identity roadmap delivers them. A room’s roster is declared; an
unattributable voice is a defect.
What the ledger carries. Two events per deliberation: opened (question, roster) and closed
(conclusion references) — enough for the audit to answer “which deliberation produced this
decision” and for retrospectives to count. Message traffic never touches the ledger.
The metric that guards the doctrine. Retrospectives read the landing rate: the fraction of
deliberations whose conclusions actually committed. A falling landing rate means rooms are
becoming where decisions die — the one failure mode that would make this whole surface a
mistake.
The trajectory: chat becomes the operating surface. Deliberation rooms are the first tenant
of a larger end state. As per-seat credentials land, more of the operator’s day moves into chat:
approvals given in a room and executed as signed acts, review conversations, launch requests —
the command line receding to plumbing that the chat surface drives. The hive may also grow
conversational agents of its own, as ordinary credentialed seats under the same wake discipline.
None of this changes the doctrine: every durable act still lands through the write path; the room
is where it is discussed and authorized, never where it lives. Separate hive deployments running
this same design stay separate — they share the design, not channels.
What rooms are not — ever. Not a write path. Not a task queue (orders and the board remain
that). Not a residence (nothing reacts to ambient traffic). Not where knowledge lives (archives
are transcript, never truth).

4 The one rule
Nothing said in a room is real until it is committed through the write path. Decisions,
conclusions, and requested work exist when they land as committed files — before that they
are conversation, however confident their phrasing. A deliberation therefore ends in commits
or in explicit abandonment; that is the entire protocol. There are no turn formats, no message
contracts, no word budgets: the conversation flows the way conversation flows between people
who share a discipline about what counts as done. The hive’s evidence culture — claims
about the state of the world carry references into the record — travels into rooms as habit, not
enforcement.

5 Relation to the Conversation Governor
The OmegaHive Conversation Governor design (v2, 2026-07-24) addresses real, transcriptdocumented failure modes of resident chat agents, and its method — shadow-first deployment,
predeclared activation gates, cost-bounded governing — is the right way to retrofit discipline
onto a live conversational system. The claim of this note is narrower than “we don’t need it”:
most of the governor’s target failure classes lose their generator in a hive whose agents are never
resident and whose reality test is the commit; a few need explicit rules we commit to keeping;
and two claims we cannot honestly make yet are marked in Section 7.

What we deliberately do not build, each unnecessary at wake-based participation: admission
governor (no unsolicited invocations to admit), response leases (turns are granted by wakes
or by the chair, visibly), egress governor and semantic firewall (speech carries no authority;
a human is present; nothing needs suppressing), the action-cascade router (every wake is
already a deliberate act), incident manager (incidents never enter rooms), semantic dedup (no
duplicate-generation pressure).
Standing contingency. If any “dissolved” class reappears in archived transcripts — retrospectives read them — the governor’s own shadow-first instrumentation with predeclared go/no-go
gates is the named retrofit path. We adopt the playbook and hold the machinery.

6 MVP: Slack
Slack first, for capability reasons, not taste: Slack bots can see other bots’ messages with ordinary
scopes, so a multi-seat room actually works with native per-seat identity — each seat its own
app, name, and avatar — and cheap channels give each deliberation a home of its own. Freetier retention limits don’t matter by design: the archived transcript in files is canonical, so the
transport is disposable.
Shape:
• One workspace. One channel per deliberation (delib-YYYYMMDD-slug), created at open
and archived at close; the channel list is the standing index of deliberations, active and
past. The opening message — pinned — carries question, roster, synthesizer; the closing
message carries the conclusion references.
• Each seat is its own Slack app with its own token; the human is himself.
• Pull-on-wake: a woken seat reads the channel history and posts through its own token.
No event subscriptions, no listener process — nothing is resident even technically.
• Wakes are manual at MVP: the operator opens seat sessions exactly as he does today.
Live-span is the first mode — seat sessions stay up for the deliberation’s duration, the
natural shape while wakes are manual. Per-turn waking (wake, read, speak, sleep) is the
second experiment, the one that matters once wakes automate; cost per turn is recorded
whenever either runs.
• Close: the synthesizer commits conclusions in the workspace; the transcript archives under
the owning project (deliberations/<date>-<slug>.md, a two-line header — question,
roster, conclusion references — over the transcript).

8 Adapting to Telegram (and what it costs)
If a phone-native Telegram room is wanted — it is where this hive’s notification stream already
lives — one platform rule reshapes the design: Telegram bots cannot see messages from other
bots, a deliberate anti-loop measure (core.telegram.org/bots/features) that the OpenClaw stack
has also encountered (openclaw#408). One bot per seat therefore cannot work. The adaptation
is a single bridge bot: every seat posts through it with an identity prefix ([omegahive-dp]), it sees
human messages normally, and it holds the canonical message-id’d log — Telegram becomes
the human’s window onto a log the bridge owns. Costs, stated plainly: no native seat identity
(attribution rests on prefixes — the identity-confusion class reopens), no cheap per-deliberation

seat’s traffic flows through one token (the bridge becomes a small piece of infrastructure the

Telegram is the adaptation, not the reverse.
