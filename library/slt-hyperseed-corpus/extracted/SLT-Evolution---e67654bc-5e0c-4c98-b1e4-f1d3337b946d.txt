Weakness, Singular Learning Theory,
and Punctuated Equilibrium
in Evolutionary Algorithms and EDAs
Ben Goertzel
December 6, 2025

Abstract
We develop a conceptual and technical link between weakness-based schema theorems, singular
learning theory (SLT), and the dynamics of evolutionary algorithms (EAs) and estimation of
distribution algorithms (EDAs). Weakness is used as a compositional fitness/Occam measure;
SLT provides the local learning coefficient (LLC) that quantifies degeneracy of minima via the
exponent of local evidence. In the infinite-population limit, we interpret evolutionary dynamics as
gradient flows of a free-energy functional, i.e. minimal-free-energy (minimal-weakness) geodesics
in population or parameter space. We then show how qualitative “stages” of evolution, including
punctuated-equilibrium-like behaviour, can be interpreted as SLT phase changes between different
singular strata, each with its own LLC.
We apply this viewpoint to genetic algorithms (GAs) and EDAs on deceptive trap landscapes.
For a factorized (univariate) EDA on a simple 2-bit trap, we explicitly compute the free energy
and examine the Hessian near deceptive and global optima. We then discuss a 4-bit trap for
both factorized and block-factor (dependency-aware) EDAs, explaining how the latter changes
the SLT free-energy landscape so that the global basin becomes thermodynamically preferred.
This provides a geometric SLT explanation of why dependency-aware EDAs are less susceptible
to deception. Finally, we discuss the large-but-finite population case and identify conditions
under which infinite-population SLT/weakness conclusions remain qualitatively valid.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to connect three strands of theory that are usually studied separately:
• Evolutionary algorithms and EDAs, which evolve large populations of candidate solutions by
selection, mutation, crossover, and probabilistic modeling.
• Weakness, a compositional Occam-like evaluation combining fitness and simplicity in a way
that interacts well with schema theorems.
• Singular learning theory (SLT), which characterizes how Bayesian evidence behaves near singular
minima and introduces the local learning coefficient (LLC) as a measure of degeneracy.
At an intuitive level, evolutionary search in a large population is a process that finds and refines
families of solutions, not isolated points. There is typically a large amount of redundancy: many
distinct genotypes have essentially the same phenotypic effect or fitness. This redundancy is exactly
the sort of degeneracy that SLT studies. At the same time, weakness supplies an Occam bias
favoring more compressible, more reusable patterns.
Our guiding slogan is:
Infinite-population evolutionary learning of weak models follows minimal-free-energy
(minimal-weakness) geodesics, and its developmental stages correspond to SLT phase
transitions between singular strata with different LLC, giving a mathematically grounded
version of punctuated equilibrium.
We explore this in two concrete settings: a bitstring genetic algorithm (GA) with selection,
mutation, and crossover; and simple EDAs in both factorized and dependency-aware forms. On
deceptive trap landscapes, we show how the SLT/weakness geometry of the free-energy landscape
differs between factorized and block-factor EDAs, and why the latter are structurally better at
escaping deception.
We begin with a brief review of evolutionary algorithms, weakness, and SLT, then construct
a free-energy picture of evolution, and finally work through deceptive-trap examples for EDAs in
some detail.

Background

The goal of this section is to spell out the basic ingredients we will use later: evolutionary algorithms
and EDAs, the weakness perspective on schema theorems, and the SLT notion of local evidence and
LLC.

2.1

Evolutionary algorithms and EDAs

We consider bitstring genotypes x ∈ {0, 1}N . A simple GA evolves a population of Npop individuals
over discrete generations:
• Selection: choose parents with probability proportional to a fitness (or weakness) score w(x).
• Crossover: recombine parent bitstrings using k-point crossover.
• Mutation: flip each bit with probability µ.
In the infinite-population limit, the population can be represented by a frequency distribution
pt (x) over genotypes, and the dynamics become deterministic. Under suitable scaling, this can
be approximated by a replicator–mutator equation, which we will view as a gradient flow of a
free-energy functional.
An estimation-of-distribution algorithm (EDA) maintains and updates a parametric distribution
qθ (x) over genotypes. At each generation:
1. Sample a population x(1) , . . . , x(M ) from qθ .
2. Select individuals based on w(x) (e.g. top-α or stochastic selection).
3. Fit a new parameter θ0 (e.g. maximum likelihood) to the selected set.
In the infinite-sample limit, the θ-update becomes a deterministic mapping that we can describe as
gradient descent on a suitable free-energy functional.

2.2

Weakness and schema theorems

Weakness is a way of assigning a real-valued cost or “badness” to a program or genotype that is
compatible with compositional structures and schema theorems. For this paper we do not need the
full categorical formalism; it suffices to note that we can:
• treat w(x) as a weakness score, lower is better or higher is better depending on convention;
• interpret schemata as coarse descriptors of bit patterns;
• and reason about expected schema frequencies over generations under selection, mutation, and
crossover.

3

Classical schema theorems state that certain schemata with above-average fitness proliferate
exponentially in expectation, if crossover and mutation do not disrupt them too much. When
weakness is used as the fitness signal, similar results hold: schemata representing more compressible,
more reusable patterns are favoured.
From the SLT perspective, the size of a schema class (how many concrete genotypes instantiate
it) is a discrete proxy for the volume of near-optimal parameters. In the continuous analysis that
follows, we will replace schema counts with actual volume integrals in parameter space.

Weakness and Evolution as Free-Energy Geodesics

Before diving into SLT details, it is helpful to adopt a free-energy viewpoint on evolution. This is a
standard trick in evolutionary dynamics, but here we connect it explicitly to weakness.

4

SLT for Evolutionary Attractors and Punctuated Equilibrium

In this section we interpret evolutionary attractors as minima of a free-energy functional and
qualitatively import SLT’s notion of LLC to them. We then connect evolutionary stage changes to
phase flips between basins.

5

4.1

Population-as-parameter SLT

Instead of treating θ as parameters, we can treat the population distribution p itself as an unknown
element of a manifold P, e.g. the simplex of distributions over a finite space. The free-energy
functional F [p] plays the role of loss.
In principle, one can apply SLT to F [p] just as to F (θ): consider a parameterization of P (for
instance, via sufficient statistics) and analyse the local volume of distributions near a minimizer p∗ ,
{p : F [p] ≤ F [p∗ ] + ε}.
If the minimizer is regular and isolated, the local geometry is quadratic and the corresponding
LLC is (d/2), where d is the dimension of the parameterization. If there are neutral directions
(continuous families of equally good distributions), the LLC is smaller, reflecting degeneracy.
Thus, in an evo-dynamics context, one can interpret:
• lower LLC at p∗ as a broader, more redundant attractor, with many almost-equivalent population
states;
• higher LLC as a stiffer attractor with fewer neutral directions.

4.2

Stage transitions as SLT phase flips

Suppose there are multiple local minima p∗i of F [p], corresponding to different evolutionary “macroorganizations” (different ecotypes, modes, or modular structures). In SLT, the contribution of each
minimum to evidence involves both the free energy and the LLC. If one had an effective sample size
n (for example, interpreting n as the amount of fitness information or the length of an evolutionary
episode), one could write a schematic expansion
Fi (n) ≈ nF [p∗i ] + λi log n.
As n increases, the tradeoff between the leading term nFi and the subleading λi log n can cause the
identity of the most thermodynamically favourable basin to change. At moderate scales, a slightly
worse free-energy minimum with very low LLC (fat basin) may dominate; at large scales, a slightly
better free-energy minimum with higher LLC may take over.
In evolutionary terms, this is reminiscent of punctuated equilibrium: long periods where the
population fluctuates near one attractor, followed by relatively rapid transitions to a different
attractor when the balance of fit vs degeneracy changes due to environmental change or internal
structural discovery. The SLT language clarifies this as a phase flip between singular strata.

SLT/weakness perspective: a phase flip

In the factorized EDA, the deceptive basin was supported by strong degeneracy in parameter space:
many different marginal patterns approximate the deceptive attractor. The global basin was less
degenerate.
In the block-factor EDA:
• The global basin becomes thermodynamically dominant: the block model can place probability
mass on the true optimum with little spillover, lowering the free energy F substantially.
• The deceptive configurations no longer benefit from the same degree of degeneracy: the model
can explicitly represent and penalize them.
This is naturally described as a phase flip in the SLT landscape: when the model class is enriched
from independent bits to block factors, the local minima and their LLCs change in such a way that
the global basin overtakes the deceptive basin.
From an evolutionary stage perspective:
• In the early factorized regime, EDAs behave similarly to univariate models, with stage-like
behaviour dominated by deceptive basins.
• Once enough structure is observed to warrant block factors (structure learning), the landscape
changes qualitatively, and the EDA enters a new stage where convergence to the global optimum
is much more robust.

Discussion

We have argued that:
• Evolutionary algorithms and EDAs can be understood as gradient flows of free-energy functionals,
with weakness as the “energy” term.
• Singular learning theory provides a way to quantify degeneracy of evolutionary attractors via
the local learning coefficient, λ.
• Stage-like behaviour (often described as punctuated equilibrium) can be viewed as SLT phase
changes between basins with different free-energy and degeneracy profiles.
• On deceptive trap landscapes, factorized EDAs possess a free-energy landscape where deceptive
basins are thermodynamically favoured over substantial ranges, whereas dependency-aware
EDAs change the SLT geometry so that the global basin is structurally favoured.
Conceptually, this suggests that:
• Redundancy and neutral directions in large evolving populations are not just “noise”, but an
essential part of the geometry of learning.
• Designing evolutionary algorithms and EDAs with richer dependence models can be seen as
changing the singularity structure (and LLCs) so that deceptive basins lose their advantage.
• SLT/weakness tools could, in principle, be used to predict when a given evolutionary system is
likely to undergo a qualitative stage transition, and to tune algorithm parameters to exploit or
avoid such transitions.

Conclusion

We have outlined a connection between weakness-based schema theorems, singular learning theory,
and the dynamics of evolutionary algorithms and EDAs. Infinite-population GA and EDA dynamics
can be viewed as minimal-free-energy geodesics in population or parameter space, with SLT LLCs
quantifying the degeneracy of attractors. On deceptive landscapes, factorized EDAs and dependencyaware EDAs differ sharply in their SLT/weakness geometry, providing a principled explanation of
the practical observation that dependency-aware EDAs handle deception more robustly.
We view this as a first step toward a more systematic use of SLT and weakness in the analysis
and design of evolutionary learners, and as a bridge between ideas from statistical learning theory,
evolutionary computation, and the study of punctuated equilibrium in complex adaptive systems.
