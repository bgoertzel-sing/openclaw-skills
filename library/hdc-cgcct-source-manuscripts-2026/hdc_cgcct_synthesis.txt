       Hypervector Frames for Category-Guided Causal Coding:
       Merging HDC Capacity Theory with Universals-Guided Transformer Training,
                     with a Staged Small-Scale Measurement Programme

                                             Ben Goertzel

                                             July 26, 2026


                                                Abstract
         Two recently developed frameworks approach the internal organization of transformer net-
     works from opposite directions. Category-Guided Causal-Coding Transformers (CGCCT) treat
     a trained network as a quantale-enriched category of modular latent states and context updates,
     connect it to language through learned cognition-to-frame and frame-to-language maps, and use
     typological universals—implicational hierarchies, closure laws, naturality squares—as training
     signals that shape latent causal modules. The theory of resonator-factored hierarchical hyper-
     vector codes (HDC) supplies a concrete algebra of role-bound factor representations together
     with a quantitative capacity theory: coherence-aware cleanup bounds, basin-conditional res-
     onator decoding, admissibility diagnostics, and losses that push a host learner toward low-load,
     high-margin, role-stable factorizations. This note argues that the two frameworks compose:
     hypervector frame codes give CGCCT’s frame category a computable object language in which
     naturality, closure, mediator structure, do-footprints, and double-counting become differentiable
     algebraic quantities, while CGCCT gives the HDC admissibility losses a principled organizing
     grammar and a source of linguistically grounded supervision. The composition also transfers
     guarantees: CGCCT’s probe-calibration assumption is discharged by the HDC coherence-aware
     capacity law, and we conjecture that implicational hierarchies sit precisely in the law’s high-
     coherence (near-duplicate) regime, making the corrected k 3/2 –k 2 dimension scaling operative
     rather than optional. We lay out which quantities of the merged framework are measurable
     at small scale—synthetic grammars, GPT-2 small, Pythia-160M checkpoints, small multilin-
     gual models—and which are not, and we provide, as an appendix, a detailed staged testing
     programme at GPT-2-small scale written for autonomous coding agents, with validation gates,
     known-answer checks (including rediscovery of the indirect-object-identification circuit), frozen
     design choices, compute budgets, and stop conditions.

    The claim of this note is that each framework supplies exactly what the other leaves underde-
termined.
    On the CGCCT side, three load-bearing components are specified abstractly but not concretely.
The frame category FrameQ needs an object language: something that is structured enough to
support role maps, refinements, and restrictions as morphisms, yet continuous enough to train
against and to measure with. The naturality loss needs a metric d on frame and linguistic objects
and an executable implementation of the linguistic-side transforms Vc . And the closure-preservation
guarantee leans on δ-calibrated probes whose calibration is, in the original presentation, an empirical
assumption. Hypervector frame codes answer all three: typed role-filler hypervectors are the object
language; algebraic rebinding, substitution, and masking implement the morphisms and the Vc ;
similarity is the metric; and probe calibration is not assumed but bounded by the coherence-aware
capacity law, with an explicit dimension prescription.
    On the HDC side, the admissibility losses are individually motivated but collectively ad hoc:
nothing in the capacity theory says which role inventories, which factor types, or which invariances
a language system should be pressed toward. CGCCT supplies that grammar. Universals fix
which probes must exist and which implications must close; alternation phenomena fix a family of
naturality squares with known algebraic right-hand sides; the mediator and double-counting rules
fix how cross-module structure must be routed and audited. The HDC role-consistency loss turns
out to be a single naturality square; CGCCT is the functorial family it belongs to.
    The two frameworks also share their principal risk, and it is worth stating at the outset rather
than discovering later: both presuppose that a low-defect frame-level factorization of transformer
language states exists for the query families of interest. Neither the categorical machinery nor the
hypervector algebra manufactures such a factorization; both can only detect, measure, and exploit
it. What the merger adds is that the risk becomes a set of numbers with predicted parameter
dependence—naturality defect, probe margin, closure-violation rate, factorization defect—rather
than a philosophical worry. The measurement programme of Section 7 and Appendix A is organized
around measuring those numbers cheaply before committing to expensive training.

     Third, orientation is preserved, and its absence is detectable. Under a commutative binding, the

permutation ablated, the codes of argument-exchanged frames have similarity exactly 1; with it,
their similarity drops to the unrelated-pair baseline. This signature has been verified directly in the
HDC framework’s numerical studies (synthetic relation encodings, and time-reversal of real speech
token sequences, where the role-free control produced cosine +1.000 exactly); the appendix reuses
it as a wiring self-test.

or separation (summary channels, margin losses) rather than the last. Stage P1 of the appendix
tests it directly.

4.1   Implementing Vc
The naturality loss compares Λ(U
                              b c z) with Vc Λ(z).

side: Vc must be an executable transform on linguistic objects, and d must be a meaningful metric.
With hypervector frames and linguistic codes, a useful family of Vc is available immediately:

     Identity (Vc = id): paraphrase, register change, and translation pairs assert that the frame
      code is preserved while the linguistic code varies (paraphrase) or that designated linguistic
      features vary while frame content is fixed (translation). These are contrastive constraints
      requiring no annotation beyond the pairing.

     Known rebind: argument alternations—active/passive, dative shift, causative/inchoative,
      symmetric-predicate exchange—correspond to specific role remappings. The transformed
      sentence’s frame code should equal the original’s with an algebraic slot swap applied; its
      linguistic code should differ by known feature substitutions (voice, case-frame). The right-
      hand side of the naturality square is computed, not learned.

     Closure addition: contexts that add material (a temporal adjunct, a further conjunct)
      correspond to adding bound terms; naturality asserts the old content survives unbinding
      after the addition, which is precisely the HDC crosstalk discipline with the addition counted
      in k.

    The HDC framework’s role-consistency loss—for content c appearing under roles ra and rb ,
penalize ∥ra† ⊗ za − rb† ⊗ zb ∥—is exactly the naturality square for the known-rebind Vc with a one-
slot remap. CGCCT names the functorial family; hypervectors make each member differentiable
and cheap.

4.3    Where supervision comes from
The economically important observation is that the supervision for the frame layer largely already
exists in unannotated or lightly annotated form: paraphrase corpora and backtranslation give
identity squares; templated alternation batteries (generated programmatically over verb frames)
give known-rebind squares with exact right-hand sides; morphological analyzers give linguistic-side
feature codes for closure probes in richly inflected languages. None of this requires full semantic
parsing, which is what makes a GPT-2-small-scale programme feasible at all.

5.1    Footprint codes

     Double-counting is coherence. Two modules with redundant causal roles have high
      ⟨Zm , Zn ⟩/D; the double-counting penalty is a coherence penalty on the footprint dictionary

     Mediator structure is the bridge budget. The CGCCT mediator rule—no dense A ↔ B

      smallest typed relation load whose removal severs the measured do-dependency. Mediator
      leakage likewise becomes addressable: content flowing through the mediator channel that
      fails to decode as typed relations is, by construction, leakage, and it is enumerable by cleanup
      rather than inferred by probing.

6    The weakness quantale gets a cost coordinate
CGCCT’s selection principle is multi-objective: among structures matching the data, prefer the
weakest, where weakness is valued in a product quantale over fit, retention, commutator defect,
leakage, double-counting, and cost. The HDC capacity proxy CHDC of Eq. (3) is a natural occupant
of the cost-and-confusion coordinates: it is a single measurable scalar that increases with effective
load, confusable alternatives, and dictionary coherence, and decreases with margin—exactly the
resource profile that “prefer one sparse mediator over redundant correlation” implicitly prices.
Read this way, the HDC-guided training objective of the capacity framework is a scalarization of
the CGCCT weakness order restricted to its representational coordinates, and the two selection
procedures can share bookkeeping: the same measured (k eff , M conf , ν, ∆) tuple feeds both.
    Two practical corollaries. First, weakness comparisons between candidate module structures be-
come cheap enough to run inside a training loop, because every coordinate is a vector computation
over codes already being maintained. Second, the quantale’s monotonicity requirements (composi-
tion cannot improve hom-values) are respected by construction, since similarity under composition
of algebraic operations can only degrade—a small point, but one that keeps the enriched-category
story honest rather than decorative.

8     Discussion
What the merger buys. Concretely: an object language for FrameQ whose morphisms exe-
cute; naturality squares with computed right-hand sides and free supervision; closure probes whose
calibration is a capacity theorem with a dimension prescription; footprint auditing that scales as
similarity search; a shared cost coordinate for weakness selection; and reuse of episodic support
memory for the confusion graph. In the other direction: a principled inventory of which HDC
probes and invariances a language system should be trained toward, replacing an ad hoc loss menu
with one indexed by universals and alternations.

What it does not buy. The existence of a low-defect frame factorization of transformer lan-
guage states is assumed by both frameworks and guaranteed by neither. The merger converts this
assumption into measurable quantities with predicted parameter dependence, which is the most
that honest methodology can do before the measurements are made. The small-scale programme is
therefore front-loaded with instrument validation and known-answer checks, and its stop conditions
treat a well-measured null as a deliverable rather than a failure.

Relation to the broader programme. The synthesis slots into an existing lineage: the causal-
coding and HBCML/ColBaC line supplies the modular substrate and teacher machinery; the HDC
capacity line supplies the representational algebra and its budget; CGCCT supplies the linguistic
supervision grammar. The appendix’s programme is deliberately written in the same staged, gated,
agent-executable format as the causal-fibres and predictive-coding programme documents, so that
the same operational tooling—and the same standards for what counts as a validated instrument—
carry over.


A     Agent testing programme at GPT-2-small scale
This appendix is written for autonomous coding agents. Run stages in order. Each stage has a
purpose, procedure, gates, budget, and stop conditions. A cleanly measured null is a successful
outcome; the only failure is a corrupted positive. Fix only what blocks execution; record
every deviation. Frozen design choices (Section A.10) may never be changed.

A.1    Mission and non-goals
The programme validates the measurement instruments of the merged CGCCT–HDC framework
and then runs the smallest honest version of the CGCCT four-model comparison. Non-goals:
demonstrating at-scale quality preservation; open-ended generation quality; typological breadth


                                                  9
beyond English plus one templated multilingual battery. Do not benchmark generation samples;
do not report perplexity improvements as the headline (the framework predicts parity, not improve-
ment, on validation loss).

A.3    Stage P0: infrastructure and self-tests
Purpose. Stand up hooks, the HDC probe library, and wiring self-tests before any measurement.
  Procedure.

  1. Implement the frame-code library: Eq. (4) encode, role rebind, slot swap, role-mask restrict,
     algebraic probes, cleanup with margins, batched resonator. Property-test each against hand-
     computed cases.

  2. Orientation self-test: encode argument-exchanged two-role frames with and without the
     target-argument permutation. Gate: without, cosine = 1.000 exactly; with, cosine within
     ±0.05 of the unrelated-pair baseline measured on random frame pairs.

  3. Probe self-test on planted bundles: k role-bound features, probe each; reproduce the single-
     step capacity curve (accuracy vs D at fixed k, M ) qualitatively.

  4. Transformer hooks: residual-stream and per-head output capture on gpt2 at every layer;
     verify shapes and determinism (two runs, same seed, identical captures).

  5. Tiny-shakespeare smoke: end-to-end run of the P2 pipeline on a char-level model, checking
     only that nothing crashes and artifacts are written. Its numbers are not evidence; do not
     report them as such.

   Budget. < 1 GPU-hour. Stop if any self-test gate fails after two fix attempts.

Purpose. Verify that probe calibration follows Eq. (2) with ground truth available, and test

A.6    Stage P3: footprints, double-counting, and the IOI known-answer check
Purpose. Validate the footprint machinery against a circuit whose answer is known.
  Procedure.

  1. Implement patched-ablation do-footprints for all 144 attention heads of gpt2 on (i) the
     indirect-object-identification (IOI) task distribution and (ii) a neutral corpus slice; encode
     footprints as Eq. (5) codes.

  2. Cluster footprint codes by cleanup/ANN; compute the double-counting coherence matrix over
     heads; extract the top redundancy groups.

  3. Known-answer check: the head groups identified for the IOI distribution must substantially
     recover the published IOI circuit structure (name-mover heads, S-inhibition heads, duplicate-
     token heads), including at least one redundancy group corresponding to the known backup
     name-mover phenomenon. Verify the reference decomposition against the literature before
     scoring—the drafting model’s recall of the circuit membership must not be treated as ground
     truth.

  4. Mediator probe: estimate minimal typed-relation budgets kB between the recovered groups
     and compare against direct dense-coupling estimates.

    Gates. Head-group recovery overlaps the verified published circuit with Jaccard ≥ 0.4 at the
group level; the redundancy (double-counting) detector flags the known backup heads. If recovery
fails, debug the footprint estimator on synthetic planted circuits (two heads with forced redundant
roles) before concluding anything.
    Budget. 6–12 GPU-hours (ablation sweeps dominate).

A.7    Stage P4: closure probes on natural text
Purpose. Move closure measurement from planted to natural hierarchies, within English’s limits.
    Procedure. Using K   b from P2 and morphosyntactic feature codes from an analyzer or tagger:
measure closure-violation rates for agreement-related implications and animacy/definiteness effects
on held-out corpus text, at the D prescribed by the P1-fitted law; include a deliberately false
implication as a negative control (its “violation rate” must be high). Optionally repeat one battery

cannot show. Gate. True implications violate at < 5× the P1-predicted probe-noise floor; the
false-implication control violates at > 20× that floor. Budget. 2–4 GPU-hours.

A.8    Stage P5: the four-model comparison in miniature
Purpose. The smallest honest version of CGCCT’s central experiment: does category-guided
training deliver matched validation loss with lower commutator defect, double-counting, and for-
getting?
    Procedure.

  1. Define C = 6 context streams over gpt2-scale data (e.g. genre/domain slices), trained sequen-
     tially via LoRA adapters plus a small shared trunk unfrozen; two stream orders (forward,
     reversed).

  2. Train four variants with matched budgets: (1) baseline; (2) modular routing without linguistic
     losses; (3) causal-coding losses (mediator, double-counting, commutator with HDC overlap
     weights) without frame supervision; (4) full: (3) plus frame-layer naturality and closure losses
     from P2/P4 machinery.

  3. Metrics per variant: validation loss per stream; commutator defect on the P3-derived confu-
     sion graph; double-counting coherence; forgetting (loss regression on earlier streams); natu-
     rality defect; closure-violation rate; and the weakness-quantale coordinates including CHDC .

  4. Three seeds minimum; report per-seed numbers, not just means.

    Gates and reading. The framework’s prediction is: validation loss within noise of baseline
for (3) and (4), with monotone improvement (1)→(4) on commutator defect, double-counting, and
forgetting, and with (4) beating (3) on naturality and closure specifically. Any variant that improves
auxiliary metrics by sacrificing validation loss beyond noise has failed the parity requirement and
must be reported as such. Budget. 1–3 GPU-days; this is the expensive stage and is gated on
P1–P2 passing.
