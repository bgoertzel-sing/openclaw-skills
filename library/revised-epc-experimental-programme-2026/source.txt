                               Causal Fibres / Hyperon ePC effort

                                             July 23, 2026

                                                Abstract
          This document restructures the ePC / causal-fibres experimental programme in light of
      two external results due to Mesto (2026): (i) homotopy distillation of GPT-2 (124M) into an
      error-parameterised predictive-coding (ePC) network, near-losslessly and reproducibly, via a
      backpropagation-anchored deformation schedule; and (ii) a companion demonstration that the
      same transformer’s forward pass runs natively inside the MORK symbolic store, with a strongly
      rank-concentrated PC error field (top 5% of error cells carrying ≈ 90% of squared update mass).
      Three structural changes follow for our programme: the direct-absorption stage previously
      abandoned after the R8/R9 collapse is replaced by a guarded homotopy variant and re-run
      first (E1); frontier-stability measurement is promoted to the keystone experiment deciding the
      emergence question (E2); and MORK integration, previously a long-horizon item, is pulled
      forward (E5). Two further experiments test whether fibre structure earns its keep over raw
      rank concentration (E3) and whether any teacher-free settle objective closes the amortization
      gap (E4). All experiments run on the six-layer synthetic-grammar rig, where ground-truth
      generative factors and genuine do-operations are available. This document specifies, for each
      experiment: hypothesis, setup, conditions, metrics with formulas, pre-registered acceptance
      criteria, branch logic, and implementation notes.

1     Context: what changed and why the plan is being revised
1.1   Prior internal results assumed known
The reader (human or agent) is assumed to have access to the causal-fibres synthesis report and
sandbox codebase. The facts this plan builds on:
 1. R8/R9 (absorption collapse). Direct ePC distillation of the six-layer GPT-2-style teacher
    under energy-monotonicity training concentrated update credit in the final block and col-
    lapsed representation rank. This motivated the frozen-backbone + sidecar architecture of
    the causal-fibres design.
 2. S2 (energy descent ̸= causal correctness). Energy minimisation can coexist with
    causally wrong credit assignment; correctness must be established by interventional diag-


                                                    1
      nostics, not by energy curves.
 3. S3/S4 (supplied-structure successes). With supplied supports and supplied fibres in
    canonical coordinates, fibre-local plasticity gives zero unrelated drift, and transports must
    be identified separately from routing. All positive sandbox evidence lives in this supplied-
    structure regime; nothing yet tests emergence of fibre structure from a trained model.
 4. v0.3 JBD. The robust joint block diagonalizer recovers hidden common block structure from
    context-conditioned operators and flags repeated-copy underidentification.

1.2    External input: Mesto (2026)
Two results, treated here as provisional but load-bearing:
 1. Homotopy distillation at 124M. A pretrained GPT-2 (124M) is deformed into an ePC
    network along a schedule τ = ηT , T ∈ {1, 2, 4, . . . , 128}, anchored at the exact BP identity
    at T =1 (at ε = 0 the forward pass is the vanilla network and one relaxation step reproduces
    the backpropagation adjoint). Across five runs, student–teacher prompt KL stays near 10−5
    nats at every milestone; held-out perplexity within 0.4%; per-block cosine to teacher ≥ 0.998;
    while the mean per-block error-norm ratio rises 0.05 → 0.98.
 2. Error-mass concentration. At the terminal settle, the top 5% of the 4.72M error cells
    carry ≈ 90% of squared update mass; fixed-depth Hill statistic α ≈ 1.68 at the top-5% tail,
    nearly τ -invariant; no power-law plateau under tail-depth sweep.
 3. MORK companion. The full GPT-2 (124M) forward pass runs natively in the MORK
    trie-backed store to 6.06 × 10−6 maximum relative logit error, with KV-caching derived as
    incremental view maintenance and a blocked tensor format giving a 16× expression-count
    reduction.
Known open ambiguities in Mesto’s report (communicated to the author; our own experi-
ments must not inherit them):
   Clamped vs. deployed. The settle energy’s loss term is the distillation KL to the frozen teacher,
    so all reported fidelity numbers are measured with teacher access during settle. The teacher-
    free ε=0 feedforward fidelity of the terminal-τ student is unreported. Every fidelity metric
    in this plan is therefore reported in both clamped and teacher-free conditions, always.
   Depth distribution of the frontier. Concentration is reported over cells but not over blocks;
    whether the top-k frontier piles into late layers (the R8/R9 pathology in slow motion) is
    unknown. Every frontier measurement in this plan is reported per block as well as pooled.

1.3    The three structural changes
 1. Absorption returns, homotopy-guarded (E1). The frozen-backbone compromise was
    forced by R8/R9. If a BP-anchored homotopy with fidelity guards avoids the collapse on our
    rig, absorption stops being the dangerous last stage.
 2. Emergence becomes the keystone (E2). Mesto’s concentration result supplies the raw
    material (a small update frontier) but not the structure (stable, context-indexed subspaces).
    Whether fibres emerge from a homotopied error field, under the most favourable conditions



                                                 2
      that will ever exist (few layers, few factors, ground-truth do-operations), decides the pro-
      gramme’s central claim.
 3. MORK moves forward (E5). The store demonstrably hosts a model 20× larger than
    ours. Hosting the six-layer PC model in MORK de-risks the long-term target and supplies
    the substrate in which local credit assignment is an engineering constraint rather than an
    aesthetic preference.


2     Shared platform, notation, and methodology standards
2.1     The rig
All experiments use the existing six-layer GPT-2-style transformer rig trained on the synthetic
compositional grammar with F ground-truth generative factors (F = 4 in the current configura-

     a frozen teacher T (the trained six-layer model);

2.3     Homotopy schedule and guards (shared by E1–E3)
     Schedule: T ∈ {1, 2, 4, . . . , Tmax } geometric; effective relaxation time τ = ηT , η fixed within
      a run; start Tmax at 128 and reduce if the rig converges earlier.
     Stage advance requires an energy-convergence hold criterion (energy-convergence ratio below
      threshold for H consecutive evaluations); on failure, subdivide the τ step.
     Divergence-abort guard: abort a run if clamped prompt KL to teacher exceeds a threshold
      fixed in advance. Log guard status every milestone; report whether the guard ever fired.

2.4     Methodology standards (apply to every experiment)
These adopt the replicate-campaign lesson from Mesto §4 and our own S-series practice. They are
mandatory; agents must implement them as shared harness code, not per-experiment ad hoc
scripts.
 1. No thresholds from single runs. Acceptance thresholds are set as the strict envelope
    over a replicate set of n ≥ 3 runs, under a rule fixed before the final replicates are inspected.
 2. Matched seeds for comparisons. Any A-vs-B configuration comparison runs both arms
    at identical seeds and is judged on per-seed deltas.
 3. Pre-registration. Each experiment’s acceptance criteria (Appendix ??) are frozen in the
    repo (versioned JSON) before the first full run; deviations are logged, not silently applied.
 4. Dual-condition fidelity. Every fidelity metric is reported both clamped (settle includes
    teacher loss) and teacher-free (ε=0 feedforward, no settle, no teacher anywhere in the evalu-
    ation path). The harness must make the teacher-free path a first-class evaluator, asserted to
    touch no teacher weights.
 5. Per-block reporting. Any statistic over error cells is emitted both pooled and per block ℓ.
 6. Interventional validation. Any claim of causal credit correctness is validated against
    do-operation ground truth, never against energy descent (S2).
 7. Artifacts. Every run emits: config hash, seed, git commit, per-milestone metrics (JSONL),
    stored error-field histograms, and the standard figure set (§??).


3     E1: Homotopy re-expression of the six-layer teacher (H-R8)
3.1     Question and hypothesis
Does a BP-anchored homotopy with fidelity guards avoid the R8/R9 pathology (final-block credit
concentration and representation-rank collapse) that direct energy-monotonic ePC distillation
produced on the same rig?
Hypothesis 1. Under the homotopy schedule, the six-layer student reaches terminal τ with (i)
teacher-free ε=0 fidelity within the acceptance envelope, (ii) a depth-distributed update frontier,
and (iii) no representation-rank collapse relative to the teacher.




                                                    4
3.2   Setup
   Teacher: frozen six-layer rig model. Student: initialised from teacher, ePC parameterisation,
    L = KL(student ∥ teacher) at distillation temperature 2.
   Homotopy and guards as in §2. Replicates: n ≥ 5 (2 seeds × 2 microbatch shapes + pilot),
    matching Mesto’s design so results are directly comparable.
   Contrast arm (required): re-run the original R8/R9 recipe (direct energy-monotonic train-
    ing, no homotopy anchor, no guards) at matched seeds, same token budget, as the pathology-
    positive control. If the historical run is reproducible from the archived config, reuse it;
    otherwise rerun.

3.3   Diagnostics
D1 (teacher-free fidelity).
   At every milestone and at terminal τ : prompt KL and held-out task metric for (a) clamped
   settle, (b) ε=0 feedforward. Primary result is (b); (a) is the sanity ceiling.
D2 (frontier depth histogram).
   At terminal τ : fraction of top-k update-mass cells per block, k at the 5% depth, plus the full
   per-block mass shares. Compare against the contrast arm’s histogram.
D3 (representation rank).
   Per
    P block:Peffective rank (participation ratio of the activation covariance spectrum, PR =
   ( i λi )2 / i λ2i ) of settled states, tracked across τ , compared to teacher and to the contrast
   arm.
D4 (concentration curve).
   Cumulative squared-update-mass vs. rank-ordered cell fraction (Mesto Fig. 3 analogue),
   pooled and per block; fixed-depth Hill statistic at top-5% with model-free sampling error,
   plus the tail-depth sweep. Purpose: establish whether the 124M concentration phenomenol-
   ogy reproduces at six layers, which calibrates how far the rig generalises.

3.4   Acceptance and branches
Acceptance thresholds per §2 standards; provisional targets in Appendix ??.
Branch 3.1 (E1-A: homotopy clean). D1(b) within envelope, D2 depth-distributed (no block
holds > 50% of top-k mass; TODO: freeze exact bar pre-run), D3 no collapse. Consequence: the
frozen-backbone compromise is dissolved for this rig; E2 and E3 run on the homotopied whole
model; sidecar variants become ablations rather than the primary architecture.
Branch 3.2 (E1-B: pathology in slow motion). D1(b) passes but D2/D3 show late-block con-
centration or rank decay trending with τ . Consequence: absorption remains unsafe; the sidecar
architecture is necessary, not merely cautious; E2 runs on the sidecar error field instead; publish
the negative result with the contrast arm.
Branch 3.3 (E1-C: clamped-only fidelity). D1(a) passes but D1(b) fails badly. Consequence:
function is held jointly by weights + teacher-clamped settle; E4 (deployment settle) is promoted
to run immediately after E1, since nothing downstream is deployable without it; report the finding
to Mesto as the resolution of the ambiguity flagged in §1.2.

4     E2: Frontier stability and fibre emergence (keystone)
4.1    Question
All prior positive fibre results are supplied-structure. E2 asks, on the terminal E1 student (or
E1-B sidecar): does the settled error field contain emergent fibre structure — context-stable
supports, common invariant subspaces recoverable by JBD, aligned with the grammar’s ground-
truth factors? This is the emergence question posed under the most favourable conditions that
will ever exist; a negative here bounds the programme’s ambitions honestly.

4.2    Measurements
All computed on settled error fields over a held-out probe set with balanced context signatures,
with matched-size random-cell and shuffled-context nulls for every statistic.

   For each context signature c, the mean top-k support set Sc (cells in the top-k by squared
   update mass, majority-vote across inputs with signature c). Report the within-vs-between
   contrast

      ⇒ stable, context-indexed frontier. Report at k ∈ {1%, 5%, 20%} depths, per block and

   Replace cell sets by subspaces: per context c and block ℓ, the top-r left singular subspace

M5 (observability floor).
   The existing εobs diagnostic applied to the E1 substrate: how much of the teacher–student

4.3    Branches
Branch 4.1 (E2-A: emergence). ∆Jacc and M2 significantly above nulls, M3 recovers ≥ ⌈F/2⌉
factor-aligned blocks, M4 diagonal dominant. Consequence: first positive emergence result; the


                                                 6
recipe is homotopy first, then identification; E3 proceeds with the recovered fibres as its structured
arm.
Branch 4.2 (E2-B: concentration without structure). Concentration reproduces but stability/JBD/fingerprints
sit at null levels. Consequence: the “red regime” is real even under ideal conditions; the pro-
gramme’s honest scope contracts to supplied-structure applications (engineered fibres for engi-
neered subsystems); E3’s structured arm uses supplied fibres and is reframed as measuring the
cost of imposing structure.
Branch 4.3 (E2-C: blocked by observability). M5 shows the error field barely encodes the func-
tional gap. Consequence: the substrate, not the identification machinery, is the binding constraint;
revisit E1 configuration (deeper relaxation, alternative loss placement) before concluding anything
about emergence.


5     E3: Structured vs. unstructured frontier settle at matched bud-
      get
5.1    Question
Mesto’s proposed rank-frontier settle is our routing apparatus with the structure deleted: an
unstructured top-k gate. Does fibre structure buy anything over raw concentration — in settle
cost, in-distribution quality, or (the real bet) out-of-distribution transfer?

5.2    Conditions (all at matched settle evaluation budget)
  1. Dense: full settle, T steps, all cells (reference).
  2. Top-k: settle updates only the top-k cells by running magnitude (Mesto-style rank frontier;
     k swept over {1%, 5%, 20%}).
  3. Fibre-projected: settle updates projected onto the E2 fibres (recovered if E2-A, supplied
     if E2-B), with transports applied across blocks; plasticity gates from the S3/S4 machinery
     active.
  4. Random-k (control): settle on a random cell set of matched size.
“Matched budget” means equal total scalar update counts per token; the harness must meter and
log this, not assume it.

5.3    Evaluations
V1 (ID quality).
   Teacher-free and clamped fidelity on the ID split. Expected: dense ≈ top-k ≈ fibre ≫ random;
   a tie here is the predicted outcome, not a failure.
V2 (compositional shift).
   Same metrics on the CS split (held-out factor combinations). This is the discriminating
   condition: structured routing should degrade more gracefully than unstructured top-k if
   fibre structure has content.
V3 (transfer of routing).
   Freeze each condition’s routing/gating state on ID data; evaluate settle quality on CS data


                                                   7
      without re-estimating the frontier. Measures whether the routing function itself generalises
      — the evidence previously missing from the ladder.
V4 (continual probe).
   Sequential fine-tuning on factor-restricted streams; measure off-support drift per condition.
   Prediction from S3/S4: protection tracks gating/supports, not PC locality per se; top-k
   without supports drifts.


6      E4: Deployment settle objectives (closing the amortization
       gap)
6.1     Question
The teacher-clamped settle cannot be the deployed object. At deployment, L in Eq. (2) must
be replaced by something available at test time, or the settle must be dropped (ε=0). Does
any teacher-free settle objective beat the feedforward student? If not, the PC apparatus has no
deployment value beyond what an adapter provides, and the programme’s deployment story must
say so.

6.2     Conditions
On the terminal E1 student, evaluated teacher-free throughout:
 1. FF: ε=0 feedforward (lower anchor).
 2. TC: teacher-clamped settle (upper anchor; measures the gap available in principle, never
    deployable).
 3. OT: observed-token settle: L is cross-entropy on the observed prefix tokens (self-supervised
    settle on the prompt), free continuation.
 4. SC: symbolic-constraint settle: grammar rules expressed in MeTTa; L = λ·(constraint-violation energy from t
    queried per settle step (batched; see E5 for the in-store path). This is the Stage-4 bet made
    concrete: test-time information injected by the symbolic cap.
 5. OT+SC: combined.

6.3     Evaluation and the decisive comparison
Primary metric: task accuracy / next-token quality on ID and CS splits, plus constraint-satisfaction
rate of generated continuations. The decisive number is

per metric: the fraction of the in-principle settle gap recovered by symbolic clamping. The
grammar rules are literally the generative truth of the rig; if G ≈ 0 here, symbolic clamping will
not work anywhere easier, the amortization gap is fatal to the deployment story, and downstream
design should treat the PC form as a training-time device only. If G is material on CS in particular

7     E5: MORK hosting of the six-layer PC model
7.1   Question and scope
Mesto’s companion result hosts a 124M forward pass in MORK at 6 × 10−6 relative error with
incremental decoding. Our model is ∼ 20× smaller; hosting it, including the error field and
frontier writes, de-risks the store-native target and supplies the substrate in which local credit
is an engineering constraint. Scope for this stage: inference + settle + frontier weight-writes
in-store; from-scratch in-store training is out of scope.

7.2   Work items
 1. W1 (forward). Six-layer forward as MORK rule application over blocked tensor expressions,
    dense kernels (einsum, attention, normalisation) as sink operations, following Mesto’s blocked
    format. Fidelity target: match the PyTorch reference to ≤ 10−5 maximum relative logit error;
    exact argmax agreement on a decode probe set.
 2. W2 (settle in-store). The T -step relaxation as in-store iteration; error tensors as store
    expressions. Verify settled states match the reference settle to a pre-registered tolerance.
 3. W3 (frontier writes as IVM). Weight updates restricted to the top-k frontier, applied
    as incremental store writes; measure write volume vs. dense updates and verify the store’s
    incremental maintenance reproduces the reference update to tolerance. This is the concrete
    synergy claim (sparse frontier ↔ trie-backed incremental writes) tested rather than asserted.
 4. W4 (fibre registration). Register E2 fibres (if any) as MeTTa expressions: supports,
    subspace bases, transports, factor bindings. This is the substrate E4’s SC condition queries,
    and the interface the symbolic cap was always intended to have.
 5. W5 (cost accounting). Wall-clock and expression-count budgets for W1–W3, compared
    against the PyTorch path, reported without spin: the store will be slower; the question is by
    how much and where.


8     Controls and baselines (run alongside, not after)
C1 (BP-residual observability control).
   The standing falsifier for the interface: a plain backprop-trained residual/adapter of matched
   capacity on the frozen teacher, evaluated on every E3/E4 metric. If C1 matches the PC
   apparatus everywhere teacher-free, the parameterisation is doing no deployment work and
   the reports must say so.
C2 (dictionary-learning baseline).
   Sparse autoencoder / dictionary learning trained directly on settled error cells (per block),
   same probe set as E2. Evaluate on M3’s factor-alignment score and M4’s fingerprint selec-
   tivity. If sparse coding recovers the factors as well as JBD, the simpler method wins and



                                                 9
      the JBD machinery is repositioned as a verification tool. This baseline is mandatory in any
      write-up of E2.
C3 (matched-compute BP fine-tune).
   For any continual-learning claim (E3 V4): backprop fine-tuning at matched compute and
   matched seeds, per-seed deltas.


9     Sequencing and decision tree
Phase 1 (immediate, parallelisable): E1 (with its contrast arm) and the harness work in §2.
C1 training can start immediately (it depends only on the frozen teacher).
Phase 2: E2 on the Phase-1 substrate (choice of substrate per E1 branch). C2 runs on the same
stored error fields — the harness must persist settled error tensors for the probe set precisely so
that E2 and C2 consume identical data.
Phase 3: E3 and E4 in parallel; both consume E2’s output (fibres or the finding that there are
none). Exception: under branch E1-C, E4 jumps to Phase 2.
Phase 4: E5, overlappable with Phase 3 for W1–W2 (which need only the E1 student).
Reporting rule: each phase produces a short memo in the N-series style — established results,
boundaries stated with the same force, branch taken and why — before the next phase’s configs
are frozen.
