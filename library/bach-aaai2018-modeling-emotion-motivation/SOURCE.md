## Summary

Bach presents emotion as a cognitive-system configuration generated by
motivation and modulation. Needs create urges and urgency; changes in need
values produce pleasure or distress; anticipated rewards are weighted by
certainty, skill, and temporal discount. These signals drive primary and
attentional modulators that alter perception, memory, planning, attention, and
action selection. Affective state is emergent from this modulation, while
higher-level emotions are directed affects associated with motivationally
relevant objects or situations.

This is a strong continuous substrate for the OmegaSelf/Hyperseed proposal.
The proposed discrete emotion regime is best treated as an additional learned
or engineered macro-control layer with persistence, audit identity, and a
bounded actuator bundle—not as a replacement for Psi modulation.

## Key claims or contents

- Motivation reflects needs and produces goals/directed behavior. Emotion
  modulates perception, cognition, and action, receives valence from
  motivation and objects from cognition, and produces affective expression
  (page 45).
- Cognitive demands include competence and uncertainty reduction/novelty
  seeking, alongside physiological and social demands (pages 46–51).
- Need changes produce pleasure/distress signals that reinforce procedures and
  episodic sequences; anticipated as well as actual rewards are valenced
  (pages 53 and 69).
- The tutorial supplies explicit records and update equations for needs,
  urges, urgency, need decay, pleasure, pain, depletion pain, event certainty,
  skill, and hyperbolically discounted anticipated reward (pages 70–77).
- Psi affect is a cognitive configuration. Modulators include arousal,
  selection/securing thresholds, resolution, competence, certainty, and
  pleasure/distress-derived valence. Affective state is emergent from
  modulation; directed affects arise through associating demands with
  appetitive or aversive objects/situations (page 89).
- Emotional modulation controls the width, depth, and bias of operations over
  mental representations, affecting perception, memory, planning, and action
  selection while reducing cognitive complexity (page 90).
- The architecture distinguishes primary modulators (arousal, valence,
  aggression) and attentional modulators (resolution, suppression/selection,
  securing rate), with demand, urgency, competence, uncertainty, and obstacle
  signals feeding their dynamics (figures, pages 91–94).
- Named emotions are already causal configurations, not mere verbal labels:
  fear and anxiety combine directed affect with arousal/competence/securing;
  anger includes attributed obstruction, redirected sanctioning, arousal,
  low resolution, high action readiness, and high selection threshold;
  sadness differs by absence of an actionable obstacle and reduced action
  readiness (pages 96–98).
- Modulators have baseline, range, volatility, and duration; differing
  configurations constitute temperaments (pages 95 and 99). Motivation
  parameters are associated with personality, modulation parameters with
  temperament (page 104).
- The self is a tertiary model of the agent's modeling process; control and
  self-regulation depend on learned representations, and “the structure of
  self determines experience of emotion” (pages 128–129). Emotion objects
  require representation and motivational relevance (page 130).

## Methods or implementation details

The tutorial is a synthesis and mathematical architecture sketch, not an
evaluation paper. It specifies continuous need and modulator state, including:

- a `Need` record with state, gain/loss, decay, reward/pain sensitivities, and
  imagined-reward parameters (page 70);
- urge and urgency functions (page 71);
- need, pleasure, pain, and depletion updates (pages 72–73);
- event/consumption records and anticipated reward updates (pages 74–77);
- bounded modulator dynamics with baseline, range, volatility, and decay
  (pages 95 and 99);
- marginal aggregation of need-weighted pleasure/pain into valence (page 100);
- aggregation of need-weighted urge/urgency into arousal (page 101);
- task-specific and general competence feeding aggression/regression (page 102).

Because mathematical glyph extraction from the slide PDF is imperfect, exact
implementation should be cross-checked against the rendered pages and the
corresponding MicroPsi source/version before claiming equation-level fidelity.

## Limitations and uncertainties

- The slides do not provide empirical comparisons establishing that these
  exact equations are a validated optimal baseline. “Empirically sane” is a
  plausible engineering hypothesis, not established by this source alone.
- Several important architecture figures contain text as graphics, so plain
  PDF extraction omits details; pages 91–94 were visually inspected.
- Appraisal theories are surveyed (page 84), but the tutorial does not give a
  full formal integration of appraisal checks with the later Psi equations.
- The tutorial does not establish that a discrete regime latch is necessary.
  That is an additional OmegaSelf/Hyperseed hypothesis requiring an ablation.
- It addresses functional and experiential architecture but does not settle
  phenomenal consciousness.

## Relevance to current work

### Strong agreement

The tutorial strongly supports the core claim that emotion is a system-wide
cognitive configuration tied to motivational relevance and real action
consequences. Its need/modulator dynamics provide a principled continuous layer
for OmegaClaw, and its object-sensitive anger/sadness distinction supports
structured appraisal records rather than valence-only triggers.

### Genuine extension proposed by the regime model

OmegaSelf adds explicit, evidence-grounded self-relative appraisal. A regime
layer may add persistence/hysteresis, a stable audit identity, governance
constraints, and an explicit bounded mapping to ECAN/tool/subagent actuators.
This should be modeled as a macrostate or option over the Psi dynamics:

`m_t = F(m_{t-1}, needs_t, telemetry_t)`

`z_t = H(z_{t-1}, appraisal(Omega_t, W_t, m_t))`

`c_t = clamp(c_base + B m_t + sum_e z_{e,t} tau_e)`

where `m_t` is continuous modulation, `z_t` is optional latched regime state,
and `tau_e` is an auditable actuator bundle.

### Required evaluation

Phase 0 should reproduce a source-validated continuous Psi baseline and compare
it with the same controller plus regime latches. The decisive question is
whether latches improve long-horizon utility, switching cost, legibility, and
failure recovery beyond continuous modulation alone. If they do not, named
regimes should remain descriptive summaries rather than extra causal state.
