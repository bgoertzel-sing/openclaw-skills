                      The Heteroclinic Cap:
      Itinerant CAROM Dynamics over a Frozen Language Model
        Working note — companion to the CAROM semantics and sandbox reports

                                                 July 2026


                                                 Abstract
           A heteroclinic cap is a thin, trainable dynamical-systems layer placed on top of a frozen
       pretrained transformer. The transformer interprets a natural-language task description —
       what the subtasks are, and how they depend on one another — and its representations pa-
       rameterize a content-addressable operator machine (CAROM) whose control flow is realized
       as a stable heteroclinic channel: a chain of metastable states through which the computation
       travels, one subtask per station. The division of labor is the design thesis: the language model
       is the compiler (semantics, dependency structure, zero-shot generalization over phrasings);
       the itinerant dynamics are the runtime (persistent gated state, phase discipline, ordered
       execution, learned halting, and an interpretable execution trace). We give the reasoning
       behind the construction, its predicted strengths and failure modes, the results of a CPU
       sandbox implementation — which validated the mechanism end-to-end and isolated the base
       model’s semantic quality as the single limiting factor — and the next phase of GPU-scale
       experiments, including a second experiment in which the channel topology itself is compiled
       per-instance from the language model’s reading of dependency phrases.


1     The idea and the reasoning behind it
1.1    Two components, each supplying what the other lacks
Standalone CAROM — a library of K reusable read–transform–gate–write operators over a slot
workspace, routed by commands, executed as an itinerant flow through a heteroclinic channel
— was shown in earlier notes to be mechanically sound. Its sandbox evaluation also exposed its
two structural poverties:

(P1) Opaque commands. Routing by embedding lookup means command semantics are
     learned per-token from task reward. There is no generalization to unseen commands, and
     the realizable instruction set is bounded by K (the routing-bottleneck constraint), forcing
     carefully hand-designed command vocabularies.
(P2) A single global ordering prior. Sequential structure lives in one learned inhibition
     matrix ρ shared across all programs. Discovering per-task execution order from reward
     alone (“free-ρ”) was empirically the hard open problem: the architecture must learn one
     prior where the truth is per-instance.

    A frozen pretrained language model dissolves both, because both are language-understanding
problems wearing an architecture costume. Commands stop being tokens and become LLM-
interpreted spans: the routing mixture becomes a hypernetwork ρk = g(hm ) on the LLM

representation hm of subtask m, so a paraphrased or never-seen instruction lands near its se-
mantic neighbors and inherits their operator mixture — zero-shot routing that a lookup table
structurally cannot provide. And the inhibition matrix becomes per-instance and compiled:
ρmj = f (hm , hj ), a pairwise scorer over subtask-pair representations, reads “after the list is
reversed, double every value” as a dependency edge and writes it into the channel topology.
Slogan form: the LLM is the compiler; the heteroclinic channel is the runtime.
    Conversely, the dynamics supply what autoregressive LLM execution lacks. Chain-of-thought
has no persistent state discipline: intermediate results live in the token stream, subject to drift,
self-overwriting, recency decay, and no per-step completion guarantee; empirically, LLMs lose
track in long state-manipulation chains whose individual steps are trivial. The cap provides
exactly the missing invariants: a register workspace with gated writes (state cannot silently de-
cay); phase structure from the channel (subtask m completes — the workspace relaxes under its
constraint — before m+1 gains control); within-phase equilibrium semantics (local error correc-
tion); an absorbing halt mode (learned, variable-length termination); and a control trajectory
a(t) that constitutes a readable trace of what was executed, when, and for how long.

1.2   Predicted strengths
(S1) Zero-shot instruction generalization. Routing inherits the base model’s semantic
     geometry: new phrasings of known operations route correctly without cap retraining. The
     routing bottleneck also relaxes, because command identity is no longer the information
     channel — meaning is.
(S2) Per-instance control flow. Compiled ρ turns the free-ρ emergence problem into super-
     vised (or distilled) edge prediction: the LLM can annotate its own dependency analyses,
     converting order discovery from pure emergence into learning with an abundant, imperfect
     teacher, corrected by end-task gradients.
(S3) Execution-length robustness. The target regime is tasks bounded by state fidelity and
     sequence length rather than per-step intelligence. The cap’s accuracy should degrade more
     slowly with program length than the base model’s chain-of-thought.
(S4) Interpretability by construction. The itinerary (stations visited, dwell times), the
     routing mixtures, and the workspace snapshots form a three-level trace: which subtask
     ran, which primitives implemented it, and what it did to the state.
(S5) A learning-efficiency bonus (found, not predicted). Routing through shared rep-
     resentations couples parameters across similar commands; independent token embeddings
     do not. The sandbox found this doubles seen-command learning at matched steps — the
     cap pays for itself before the transfer question is even posed.
(S6) An arbitration reading. In the agentic variant, operators or phases invoke the LLM
     on subtasks, and the GLV competition becomes an arbitration core over agent invocations
     — activation-economics-flavored control with an explicit dynamical semantics, pointing
     toward multi-agent orchestration uses beyond single-model capping.

1.3   Predicted weaknesses and failure modes
Adapter mismatch (large, anisotropic LLM states read through thin projections; layer choice
matters; procedural content may be less linearly accessible than declarative content); the re-

dundancy trap (on tasks the base model solves alone in tokens, the cap is overhead — the case
must be made where the baseline demonstrably fails); the distillation ceiling (if the edge scorer
only learns dependencies the LLM already articulates, the cap’s contribution is discipline rather
than knowledge — acceptable, but it must be claimed as such); and inherited channel engineer-
ing (the saddle tuning package from the standalone work: entry conditions, activation floors,
fatigue, passage-time clocks).
    One further design hypothesis, due to the project’s originator: shaping the base model’s rep-
resentations with an error-predictive-coding (EPC) objective before freezing may reduce adapter
mismatch, since predictive-coding training favors states that are linearly predictive — precisely
the property a thin near-linear cap consumes — and PC inference is itself relaxation to equi-
librium, plausibly a better impedance match to a cap whose phases are constraint relaxations.
This is formulated as an A/B in the next phase.


2     Sandbox implementation and results
2.1   Setup
CPU-only container; no pretrained checkpoints available, so the frozen “LLM” is a from-scratch
stand-in: a 3-layer, d=96 causal transformer (∼0.4M parameters) pretrained by next-token pre-
diction on the task’s own text distribution, then frozen. Task: programs of 2–4 commands
over seven whole-sequence primitives on Z8 (increment, double, negate, reverse, rotate, swap,
prefix-sum), each rendered in natural language by one of three paraphrase templates; variants
0,1 train the cap, variant 2 is held out (the base pretrains on all three, unsupervised — the hon-
est miniature of “base saw all language, cap trained on a subset”). Command representations:
mean of frozen last-layer states over each command’s span. Cap models: CapScheduled (hy-
pernetwork routing, scheduled execution), TokScheduled (identical but routed from opaque
per-(primitive, variant) token embeddings — structurally blind to unseen paraphrases), and
CapItinerant (heteroclinic wrapper: LM-derived routing and fitness bias, hand-built succes-
sor chain, padded modes suppressed through the fitness channel). Single seed; small budgets;
trends only.

2.2   Results
R1: the mechanism works end-to-end. CapItinerant trains stably by BPTT through
∼56 coupled integration steps and executes 192/192 perfectly sequential itineraries at
every program length L ∈ {2, 3, 4}, with padded modes pinned at the activation floor — i.e.,
the channel runs exactly L stations for a length-L program. Variable-length heteroclinic execu-
tion over frozen-transformer routing is mechanically sound and robust to training. Accuracy is
above chance and depth-graded (0.42/0.31/0.29) at a ∼370-step budget.

R2: representation routing beats token routing on seen data. At matched steps
(∼1200), CapScheduled reached 78.5% on training paraphrases versus 41.7% for TokSched-
uled (chance 12.5%). Shared representational structure acts as an inductive bias across com-
mands; independent embeddings learn each token from scratch.

R3: transfer is systematic exactly where representation similarity exists. Held-out-
paraphrase accuracy by primitive (cap / token): shr 1.00/0.12; dbl 1.00/1.00∗ ; rev 0.35/0.16;

neg 0.18/0.10; inc 0.11/0.25∗ ; swp 0.14/0.04; cms 0.12/0.15 (∗ token-baseline successes are at-
tributable to untrained random-init embeddings landing near useful operator corners — luck,
not mechanism; hence the multi-seed requirement below). The cap transfers perfectly on prim-
itives whose held-out paraphrase shares content words with the training variants, partially on
partial overlap, and at chance on zero overlap.

R4: the base model is the isolated limiting factor. A nearest-centroid probe of the
frozen representations identifies held-out paraphrases’ primitives at only 0.27 (chance 0.14),
with a per-primitive pattern exactly mirroring lexical overlap. An attempt to induce effect-
based (semantic) equivalence by grounding the pretraining corpus with worked input/output
examples failed: the tiny model’s loss plateaued without learning to execute, so effect-based
representations never formed. Three independent measurements — the probe, the grounding
failure, and the end-to-end transfer pattern — triangulate one conclusion: the cap architecture
works; what is missing is a base that knows what the commands mean.

2.3   What the sandbox shows, and what it does not
Shown (at toy scale, single seed): the full cap pipeline trains; heteroclinic execution over
LM-derived routing is stable, sequential, and length-adaptive; representation routing dominates
token routing on seen commands; transfer tracks representation similarity with the token control
at (noisy) chance; and the limiting factor is cleanly attributed to base semantic quality rather
than to the cap.
    Not shown: semantic (non-lexical) zero-shot transfer — the central claim — because the
stand-in base cannot supply semantic similarity; any comparison against a chain-of-thought
baseline (the stand-in cannot do CoT); compiled per-instance channel topology (the sandbox
used a fixed chain); the EPC hypothesis; effects at realistic scale; and statistical robustness
(everything is n=1 seeds). The sandbox’s contribution is that the GPU phase now tests a single
isolated variable with pre-registered predictions, rather than debugging an apparatus.


3     Next phase: GPU experiments
Experiment 1: semantic transfer (base swap). Identical pipeline, frozen GPT-2-small
(124M) as base (LMD =768; span-mean pooling with BPE offset tracking; probe a small sweep of
layers). Gate: nearest-centroid probe ≳ 0.8 before training the cap. Pre-registered prediction:
held-out transfer on the zero-overlap primitives (cms, swp, inc) rises from ≈ chance toward
the seen-paraphrase level; the token control does not move. Three seeds minimum; report per-
primitive tables. Secondary: does R2’s learning-efficiency advantage grow with a semantically
organized base?

Experiment 2: the compiled channel (code delivered with this note). The qualita-
tively new capability: per-instance channel topology from dependency language. Programs are
presented scrambled, each command carrying a linguistic dependency clause (“to begin, . . . ”;
“after you reverse the order, double every value”), with distinct primitives per program so the
reference is unambiguous. A pairwise scorer smj = f (hm , hj ) over frozen span representations
predicts “m directly follows j” and is compiled into the inhibition matrix (ρmj lowered toward
the invadable value where m follows j; ρjm raised toward the extinguishing value), with a learned

entry scorer setting the initial condition from the “to begin” clause. Two supervision regimes:
(a) compiler-supervised — edge labels from the generator (standing in for LLM-annotated de-
pendency analyses; at real scale, prompt the base model for its own dependency parse) plus
end-task loss; (b) emergent — end-task loss only. Metrics: task accuracy; edge AUC; itinerary–
vs–true-order Kendall τ ; and structural length generalization — train on L ≤ 4, test on L = 5,
which the compiled channel should handle by construction (one more station in a topology it
builds anyway) and which fixed-schedule models cannot represent.

Experiment 3: EPC A/B. Fine-tune a copy of the base with the error-predictive-coding
objective, freeze, and rerun Experiments 1–2 against the standard base. Three measurable axes:
probe score (linear accessibility), cap steps-to-threshold (trainability), held-out and compiled-
channel transfer (semantic organization).

Experiment 4 (framing for later): the anti-CoT benchmark. Length-scaling curves —
capped model vs. base-model chain-of-thought on state-manipulation programs of growing L
— to substantiate S3, the regime claim. This requires a generative base configuration and is
sequenced after 1–3.


4    Closing remark
The heteroclinic cap inverts the usual neuro-symbolic arrangement. Rather than a symbolic
controller invoking a neural substrate, a neural compiler (the LLM) parameterizes a continuous
dynamical runtime whose trajectory is the program: stations are subtasks, transitions are control
flow, dwell is computation, and the trace is legible by construction. The sandbox established
that the runtime works; the next phase tests whether the compiler’s semantics flow through it.
