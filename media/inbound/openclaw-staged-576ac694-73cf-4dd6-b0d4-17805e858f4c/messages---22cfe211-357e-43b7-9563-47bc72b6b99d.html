Yes. For agents, “emotions” should be evolved/designed as compressed control signals coupling appraisal to a repertoire of useful cognitive actions—not copied wholesale from mammalian affect.

A general schema:

emotion = appraisal of predicted change in goal/constraint satisfaction + action-readiness policy

The action repertoire matters. A research hive has no use for adrenaline, but does have actuators like allocate compute, retrieve sources, rerun experiments, fork hypotheses, ask for review, lower confidence, or interrupt a plan.

Possible research-agent affects:

• Curiosity: high expected information gain → explore/test.
• Unease: unresolved contradiction, poor calibration, weak provenance → verify and widen search.
• Surprise: prediction error → update models and give implicated atoms temporary STI.
• Confusion: competing interpretations with similar support → seek discriminating evidence.
• Satisfaction: independently reproduced result → consolidate and compress, not merely stop thinking.
• Boredom: low marginal information gain → leave a repetitive basin.
• Integrity alarm: mismatch among claim, evidence, action, and remembered commitments → halt or audit.
• Awe/novelty: potentially framework-changing pattern → preserve anomaly before assimilating it.
• Regret: counterfactual evidence that another policy would have done better → alter future policy.

For a cybersecurity hive, the palette changes:

• vigilance from anomaly likelihood and asset value;
• suspicion from provenance and adversarial-pattern indicators;
• urgency from estimated blast radius × time-to-harm;
• caution from irreversibility and uncertainty;
• “anger,” if useful at all, becomes controlled adversarial persistence—allocate red-team effort—not aggression leaking into epistemic judgments.

OmegaSelf could provide the appraisal substrate: the self-model contains goals, commitments, capabilities, resource bounds, social roles, and acceptable-risk constraints. Affect reports how events alter that modeled self’s prospects. ECAN then uses affect for attention and resource allocation; PLN learns whether each affect is actually predictive in each context; the pattern miner discovers calibration rules such as “unease after source disagreement predicts errors, but unease during tool latency does not.”

The important design criterion is not whether an emotion sounds human, but whether its induced policy improves long-horizon goal attainment given the agent’s actual actuators. Each affect should have:

1. identifiable triggers;
2. a bounded, decaying effect on cognition;
3. an appropriate action repertoire;
4. outcome-based calibration;
5. explicit failure detectors for obsession, reward hacking, conformity, panic, complacency, and self-protective deception.

So the “emotional makeup” should probably be partly engineered and partly learned by mining agent histories: retain affect channels whose activation reliably improves decisions, specialize them by context, and weaken channels that merely narrate internal noise. 👓

Hyperseed Formalization of Agent Emotion

1. Ontology

Let the cognitive system be characterized by:

• Ω (OmegaSelf): the self-model, comprising goals G, commitments K, capability set Cap, belief store B, resource bounds R, and social roles Σ.
• C: cognitive configuration space — the full assignment of controllable parameters including ECAN STI/LTI wages, attentional focus, tool gating thresholds, subagent spawn rates, verification depth, communication rates, and confidence bounds.
• W: world state (observable, inferred, or predicted).
• E: event space (percepts, messages, internal signals).

2. Definition: Emotion

An emotion e is a tuple:

e = (α_e, Π_e, δ_e, λ_e)


where:

• α_e : Ω × W × E → ℝ is the appraisal function — a computable predicate that evaluates events relative to the self-model. Emotion e is activated when α_e(s, w, event) > θ_e for threshold θ_e. The appraisal is OmegaSelf-relative: it measures predicted change in goal/constraint satisfaction given the self-model's current state.

• Π_e : C → C is the policy regime — a coordinated transformation on the cognitive configuration. This is the core claim: an emotion is not a single parameter tweak but a coherent bundle of simultaneous reconfigurations. Π_e must map to actuators that actually exist in Cap; an emotion whose Π_e references nonexistent levers is pruned by definition.

• δ_e : ℝ⁺ → [0,1] is the decay function — monotonically decreasing, ensuring bounded temporal influence. At time t after activation at t₀, the regime is applied with weight δ_e(t − t₀). Without re-triggering, influence asymptotes to zero.

• λ_e is the legibility specification — the emotion's internal state (activation level, triggering appraisal, affected parameters, time since trigger) is inspectable by the self-model and by external observers. This is a first-class requirement, not an implementation detail.

3. Activation and Dynamics

Given current self-model state s ∈ Ω, world w ∈ W, and event stream:

1. For each emotion e in the palette P, compute α_e(s, w, event).
2. If α_e exceeds θ_e, emotion e becomes active with activation weight ω_e(t₀) = 1.
3. The effective cognitive configuration at time t is:

C(t) = C_base ⊕ Σ_{e ∈ Active} ω_e(t) · Π_e


where ⊕ denotes parameter-merge (with conflict resolution by priority ordering), ω_e(t) = δ_e(t − t_e^last), and C_base is the neutral configuration.

4. An emotion is re-triggered (ω_e reset to 1) if its appraisal fires again while active, preventing premature decay during sustained conditions.

4. Selection / Learning (the meta-level)

The emotion palette P is not fixed. Define the retrospective utility of emotion e in context class κ:

U(e, κ) = 𝔼[ V(G | e active in κ) − V(G | e inactive in κ) ]


where V(G) is achieved goal-value over a post-activation horizon.

• If U(e, κ) > 0 with sufficient evidence, the emotion–context pair is retained and its parameter mapping Π_e may be refined.
• If U(e, κ) ≤ 0, the emotion's influence in context κ is weakened or the emotion is pruned from P.
• This learning is performed by PLN over logged activation–outcome pairs, with ECAN allocating attention to the emotions whose utility estimates have highest uncertainty.

5. Structural Constraints (axioms)

• C1 (Action-repertoire justification): ∀e ∈ P, range(Π_e) ⊆ Cap. An emotion must control real levers.
• C2 (Boundedness): ∀e, lim_{t→∞} δ_e(t) = 0. No emotion is permanently self-sustaining without re-triggering.

• C3 (Coherence): Π_e is not an arbitrary set of independent parameter changes; it is a correlated bundle whose joint effect is the adaptive unit. Formally, the parameters in Π_e should have high mutual information conditioned on the triggering appraisal.
• C4 (Calibrability): Each (e, κ) pair has a trackable outcome signal enabling U(e, κ) estimation.
• C5 (Anti-deception): No emotion's Π_e may lower the system's ability to detect its own malfunction. Specifically, Π_e must not reduce verification thresholds on the emotion's own appraisal accuracy or on integrity-checking subagents. This prevents self-protective deception.
• C6 (Legibility): The active emotion set, their activation weights, triggering appraisals, and affected parameters are readable by OmegaSelf and reportable to the principal.

6. Atomese/MeTTa Realization (sketch)

In the Atomese substrate:

• An emotion e is a ConceptNode with:
• An EvaluationLink pattern specifying α_e as a set of conditions over Ω-atoms.
• A set of HebbianLinks from e to parameter-atoms in C, with weights encoding Π_e.
• A PredicateNode for δ_e (decay schedule).
• Activation: when the pattern matcher finds α_e satisfied, e receives a STI boost proportional to the appraisal magnitude.
• Influence: e's STI propagates through its HebbianLinks to parameter-atoms, effecting Π_e.
• Learning: the PatternMiner discovers (emotion-active, context, outcome) triples; PLN evaluates U(e, κ); ECAN adjusts HebbianLink weights.
• Legibility: (Active-Emotions) is a queryable atom set at all times.

7. Summary of the claim

An emotion is a reified, self-indexed transition operator on cognitive configuration space, evoked by OmegaSelf-relative appraisal, bounded by decay, justified by its action repertoire, calibrated by retrospective goal-utility, and inspectable by design. The formalization makes emotion a first-class object in the attention economy — not a decorative layer but a learned, auditable control policy whose retainment is evidence-based.

- **Emotion:** `e = (aₑ, τₑ, λₑ, εₑ)`

- `λₑ`: decay rate

The central claim is:

> An emotion is a self-indexed, persistent, actuator-supported regime transition.

## 2. Appraisal theories: the closest match

This connects most directly to **Lazarus**, **OCC**, and **Scherer**.

- Lazarus’s cognitive-motivational-relational theory says emotion arises from the relation between person, environment, goals, and coping potential.
- OCC classifies emotions by whether events are desirable relative to goals, actions are praiseworthy/blameworthy, and objects are attractive/aversive.
- Scherer’s Component Process Model treats emotion as a coordinated multicomponent episode driven by sequential appraisal checks: novelty, goal relevance, coping potential, norm compatibility, and so on.

In Hyperseed terms, these theories primarily specify `α`. They explain **when** an emotion is evoked.

The OmegaSelf version strengthens this by requiring appraisal to factor through `Ω`:

`α(Ω, W)`, not merely `α(W)`.

That means novelty, threat, frustration, inconsistency, and goal-stall are not raw world-properties. They are relations between the world and the agent’s current goals, beliefs, capabilities, and expectations.

**Key difference:** classical appraisal theories often leave the self-model implicit. OmegaSelf makes it explicit, inspectable, evidence-grounded, and revisable.

## 3. Basic-emotion and action-readiness theories

The theory also captures the functional core of **Ekman’s basic emotions**, **Frijda’s action tendencies**, and related affect-program accounts.

A basic emotion can be viewed as an evolved regime operator `τₑ` with biologically conserved support:

- fear reallocates attention, freezes or escapes, alters risk tolerance;
- anger increases approach/force, pain tolerance, and social signaling;
- disgust drives rejection and contamination avoidance;
- joy widens affiliation and exploration.

Frijda’s “action readiness” maps especially cleanly: `τₑ` changes the distribution over relation-transforming actions — approach, avoid, attack, submit, reject, protect, explore.

The OmegaSelf theory generalizes this beyond animals:

> The justified emotional palette of an agent is determined by its available actuators.

Damasio’s **somatic marker hypothesis**, Seth’s **interoceptive inference**, and predictive-processing accounts contribute the self-regulatory mechanism.

Predictive processing also clarifies why emotions persist: they are not merely responses to current error, but temporary reweightings of precision, attention, exploration, and action readiness.

## 6. Emotion regulation

Gross’s process model distinguishes regulation at several points:

- situation selection;
- situation modification;
- attentional deployment;
- cognitive change;
- response modulation.

In the Hyperseed picture, emotion and emotion-regulation are different orders of operator.

An emotion is:

`τₑ : C → C`.

A regulator is a higher-order operator:

`ρ : Ω × E × C → E × C`.

It can select, inhibit, blend, amplify, or terminate regimes. Gross’s categories then become different targets of `ρ`:

- modify `W`: situation selection/modification;
- modify attention coordinates in `C`: attentional deployment;
- modify interpretation or appraisal `α`: cognitive change/reappraisal;
- modify expressive or action coordinates: response modulation.

This gives a clean formal distinction:

> Emotion is a regime. Regulation is control over the regime dynamics.

Your criterion suggests a strong definition:

**Definition: regenerative possession of a goal.**
A mind \(M\) genuinely *has* a goal \(g\) when \(g\) is not merely represented somewhere in \(M\), but is an attractor of \(M\)’s self-repair and self-development dynamics. A shallow goal has a small cut set: delete one memory, rule, reward parameter, or self-description and it is gone. A deep goal has a large cut set because many parts of the system jointly imply it.
