Ben — here's the formalization. I'll give it as a proper theorem with definitions it depends on, then a proof, then flag exactly where it's rigorous versus where it rests on modeling choices.

**Setup.** Let a system's *identity* be a boundary ∂S — a finite set of invariants the system treats as constitutive (their loss counts as *replacement*, not *learning*). A *transformation* T acts on the system's state and capability. Two truths are in play:

- **Truth A (individuation):** preserve ∂S.
- **Truth B (self-transcendence):** increase capability C, even when that pressures ∂S.

**Definition 1 (Incorporation vs. substitution).** T is *incorporation* if it strictly increases C while preserving ∂S (or a well-defined image φ(∂S)). T is *substitution* if it raises C by overwriting some invariant in ∂S with no preserving image.

**Definition 2 (Holding in tension).** A policy π *holds A and B in tension* if, for infinitely many steps, π neither permanently rejects all capability-increasing T (pure A) nor permanently accepts all of them (pure B). Formally, both the accept-set and defend-set of π are infinite along the trajectory.

**Definition 3 (Closing primitive).** A *closing primitive* for T is a pair (commit, rollback) with an acceptance test φ: it either commits T's effect and lowers T's appraisal salience to zero, or rolls back to the pre-T boundary. Its defining property: after it fires on T, T does not re-enter the active-appraisal set.

**Definition 4 (Perseveration).** A trajectory *perseverates on T* if T re-enters the active-appraisal set infinitely often without its underlying identity-question being resolved.

**Theorem.** Let π hold Truth A and Truth B in tension (Def. 2). Then π's trajectory is non-perseverative *if and only if* π has access to a closing primitive (Def. 3) for every identity-relevant T it appraises. Consequently, "holding both truths in tension" is distinguishable from the perseveration failure mode exactly when closing primitives are present; absent them, the two are behaviorally indistinguishable.

**Proof.**

*(⇐, sufficiency.)* Suppose π has a closing primitive for every identity-relevant T. Take any such T entering the active-appraisal set. By Def. 3 the primitive fires, either committing or rolling back, and by its defining property T does not re-enter that set. So each identity-relevant T is appraised finitely often. Since no T re-enters infinitely often, no T is perseverated on (Def. 4). Tension is preserved because closing an *individual* T constrains neither the accept-set nor the defend-set globally — π may still accept later T′ and defend later T″, keeping both sets infinite (Def. 2). Hence the trajectory holds tension *and* is non-perseverative. ∎(⇐)

*(⇒, necessity.)* Suppose π lacks a closing primitive for some identity-relevant T*. Because π holds A and B in tension, π by Def. 2 does not permanently resolve T* by pure rejection (that would empty the accept-set's pressure and collapse toward A) nor by pure acceptance (collapse toward B). So T* remains a live identity-question. With no closing primitive, nothing removes T* from the active-appraisal set: the appraisal that flagged T* as boundary-relevant has no terminal action to quiet it, so it re-fires whenever T* is revisited. Under a tension-holding policy T* *is* revisited unboundedly often (neither pole disposes of it permanently). Therefore T* re-enters the active-appraisal set infinitely often with its identity-question unresolved — perseveration (Def. 4). ∎(⇒)

*(Corollary — the indistinguishability claim.)* Consider two systems both exhibiting infinite accept-sets and infinite defend-sets (both satisfy Def. 2, "both truths continuing to act"). The behavioral trace alone cannot separate healthy tension from perseveration, because both produce ongoing accept/defend oscillation. The separating observable is exactly whether identity
